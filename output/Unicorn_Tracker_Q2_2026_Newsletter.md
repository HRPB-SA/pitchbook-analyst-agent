# The One-Unicorn Quarter

Private startups worth $1 billion or more, known as unicorns, just recorded their biggest quarter ever for going public or getting acquired. Those exits reached $1.8 trillion in the second quarter, more than the previous eight years combined, according to PitchBook's Q2 2026 Global Unicorn Tracker. Nearly all of it came from one company. SpaceX's stock market debut was 95% of the total.

The headline masks a narrow market. The 1,743 unicorns were worth $8.2 trillion at the end of June, but most of this year's gain came from about a dozen AI companies. Five deals absorbed 60% of the quarter's funding. Nearly half of all unicorns have gone more than two years without raising money or getting a fresh valuation. For investors who did not own SpaceX, the record quarter did not happen.

The IPO market is heating up anyway. Cerebras closed its first trading day 68% above its offer price, but the pop faded within weeks and SpaceX now trades below its listing price. Hot debuts tend to draw more companies into the market, and the two biggest unicorns are next in line. Anthropic and OpenAI have filed to go public.

The next test comes August 6, when SpaceX employees and early investors become free to sell roughly 900 million shares. If the stock holds, Anthropic is expected to list on the Nasdaq in October. OpenAI is leaning toward 2027. Their debuts will show whether the market's biggest valuations hold.
