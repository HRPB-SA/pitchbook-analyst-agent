# The Unicorns Go Public

The IPO drought is over for unicorns, the private startups valued at $1 billion or more. Companies worth $1.8 trillion went public or were acquired last quarter, more than the market produced from 2016 through 2023 combined, according to PitchBook's Q2 2026 Global Unicorn Tracker. One listing did most of the work. SpaceX's debut accounted for 95% of the total.

The rest of the report tells a narrower story. The 1,743 unicorns were worth $8.2 trillion at midyear, but a dozen AI companies produced most of the gain. Five deals took 60% of the quarter's funding. Nearly half the group hasn't raised money or gotten a fresh valuation in over two years. The boom is real, and it belongs to very few companies.

The IPO window is where those valuations finally get tested. The early tests are mixed. Cerebras ended its first trading day up 68%, then gave most of it back within weeks. SpaceX has slipped below its listing price. Even so, hot debuts tend to pull more companies into the market, and the two biggest unicorns, Anthropic and OpenAI, have filed to go public.

Their timing hinges on August 6, when SpaceX employees and early investors become free to sell some 900 million shares. If the stock absorbs them, Anthropic is expected on the Nasdaq in October, with OpenAI weighing 2027. Either way, the debuts will give this market what it has lacked: a price everyone can check.
