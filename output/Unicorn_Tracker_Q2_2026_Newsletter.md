# The One-Unicorn Quarter

The second quarter was the strongest on record for unicorns, the private startups valued at $1 billion or more, and one company drove nearly all of it. Unicorn exits, companies going public or being acquired, reached $1.8 trillion, more than the previous eight years combined, according to PitchBook's Q2 2026 Global Unicorn Tracker, and SpaceX's listing accounted for 95% of the total.

The headline overstates the recovery's breadth. The 1,743 unicorns were worth a combined $8.2 trillion at the end of June, but most of the increase came from a dozen AI companies, the five largest deals absorbed 60% of the quarter's funding, and nearly half of them have not raised capital, or received a fresh valuation, in more than two years. For investors who did not own SpaceX, the record quarter did not happen.

Momentum is nonetheless building behind the IPO market. New listings jumped on their first trading day, with Cerebras closing 68% above its offer price, though the gains faded within weeks and SpaceX now trades below its listing price. Hot debuts have historically pulled more listings behind them, and the two largest unicorns, Anthropic and OpenAI, have filed to go public.

Much depends on August 6, when SpaceX employees and early investors become free to sell roughly 900 million shares. If the stock absorbs those sales, we expect Anthropic to list on the Nasdaq in October, with OpenAI waiting until 2027. Those debuts will test the valuations the whole market rests on.
