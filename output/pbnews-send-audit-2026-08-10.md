## Page 1 — What the account actually is, and what it sent

The account is not a correspondent. It is the outbound endpoint of a self-built research
bot, plus a small amount of human traffic. Almost every message is machine-generated equity
and private-market research addressed to `harrison.rolfes@pitchbook.com`. Two exceptions
exist: a run of thirteen newsletter forwards to `hypdventures@gmail.com` in April, and a
personal brokerage-application exchange with Tradier on 11 March.

The run divides cleanly into seven phases. Each is defined by which report franchises were
live, not by calendar boundaries — the pipeline was rebuilt repeatedly, and each rebuild
replaced the previous product set rather than adding to it.

| Dates | Phase | Products live | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14–17 Dec | Bootstrap | Weekly AI Deep Dive (PDF), PitchBook Morning Note, PitchBook Daily, PitchBook Intelligence: The Trust Gap | Mostly failing |
| 18–22 Dec | The $1M Morning Newsletter | $1M Morning Newsletter (10-page format), AI Analyst Morning Brief, The Rundown, PitchBook AI Coverage | First clean output |
| 24 Dec–2 Jan | Morning Alpha | /// ALPHA, /// DOSSIER (Top 150), /// BENCHMARK (Top 15 models), Master Analyst, Weekly Strategy, Daily Intel | Stale data enters |
| 2–6 Jan | Private AI Brief | Daily Private AI Brief, Weekly PE Audit, Pre-IPO Alpha, GS Research → Harrison's Report, Master Blueprint | Heavy iteration |
| 7–20 Jan | Command Center | Command Center (twice daily), Weekly PE Audit, Pre-IPO Alpha, AI 7 Deep Dive, Master Blueprint | Stable cadence |
| 21 Jan–14 Feb | AI Intelligence Brief | AI Intelligence Brief, daily at ~06:50 UTC | Best output |
| 20 Feb–27 Mar | Weekly only | Master Blueprint (Fri AM), AI 7 Deep Dive (Fri PM), Pre-IPO Alpha (1st & 15th) | Narrowing |

After 27 March the automated pipeline stops entirely. From 1 April the account produces only
manual forwards of subscription newsletters — *The Information*'s Briefing, AM, AI Agenda,
Applied AI and Dealmaker, plus The AI Collective — pushed in batches to a second personal
address. Thirteen forwards across four sittings on 1, 2, 6 and 13 April. That is the last
activity in the window.

### What each product contained

- **AI Intelligence Brief** — executive summary, top developments with significance and
  market impact, funding totals, competitive dynamics, technology trends, watchlist,
  near-term outlook, analyst take. Sourced from live news; the most substantive product of
  the run.
- **Harrison's Report: The AI 7 Deep Dive** — a conviction-list note on xAI, OpenAI, Meta,
  Databricks, Safe Superintelligence, Anthropic and Google, with a sector valuation matrix
  and per-name rationale, differentiation and key risks.
- **Master Blueprint** — a fourteen-section company dossier (identity, timeline, governance,
  capitalization, financial model, product stack, tech, data strategy, GTM, competition,
  risk, talent, forward view, FAQ) across six to twelve names.
- **Weekly PE Audit** — a forensic investment memo per company across technical moat,
  financials, operational efficiency, human capital and risk, closing with a Buy/Hold
  verdict. Six issued: Databricks (Hold), Anthropic, xAI (Buy), OpenAI (Hold), Nvidia (Buy,
  twice).
- **Pre-IPO Alpha** — a monthly thesis series: Month 1 Vertical Moats, Month 2 Unit
  Economics, Month 3 Agentic Autonomy. Each names a single winner. Nvidia won Month 1;
  OpenAI won Month 3.
- **Command Center** — a twice-daily dossier board carrying a $4.2T universe valuation,
  signal intensity and per-company moat scores.
- **/// DOSSIER and /// BENCHMARK** — a 150-company profile scan and a fifteen-model
  capability/price comparison, both issued daily over the holidays.

## Page 2 — The updates: what the reports actually reported

Read in sequence, the outbound record tells a coherent story about the AI capital cycle over
four months — accelerating megarounds through December and January, a February peak, and the
first credible cracks appearing in April.

### December 2025 — the megaround era opens

The December reports lead repeatedly on Databricks raising **$4B at a $134B valuation** on a
$4.8B revenue run-rate, growing above 55% year over year. Around it: Anthropic's **$13B
Series F at $183B**; Nvidia's acquisition of **Groq for $20B**, its largest on record;
Anysphere (Cursor) at $9B; and a long tail of mid-size rounds — d-Matrix $275M at $2B,
EliseAI $250M, Loadsmart $200M, MoEngage $180M, Terabase $130M, OpenAI-backed Chai Discovery
$130M, Odeko $126M. On the product side the briefs track OpenAI's GPT-5.2 and GPT-5.2-Codex
launches, the Disney–Sora licensing agreement, and developer app submissions opening in
ChatGPT.

The December analysis frames all of this through a single thesis — *The Constraint Economy* —
arguing that AI's trajectory is set by seven bottlenecks: compute, data, talent, distribution,
regulation, trust and capital. A companion theme, *The Trust Gap*, argues capital-trust
erosion is the binding risk and flags SaaS security credibility as the transmission channel.
That call reads well four months later.

### January 2026 — capital mobilization, then whiplash

The daily briefs from 21 January onward carry an explicit disclosed-funding total, and the
series is violently uneven: $40B on the 21st, $70B on the 22nd, three consecutive near-zero
days over the 23rd–25th, $21.8B on the 26th, then $126B on the 29th and above $100B on the
30th. The briefs read this as capital concentration rather than breadth — fewer, larger,
more strategic rounds, increasingly led by corporates rather than venture funds.

### February 2026 — the peak

Two February briefs carry the largest items in the entire record.

**5 February:** SpaceX acquires xAI in an AI–aerospace merger built around space-based
compute; Nvidia's $20B investment into OpenAI nears completion; Anthropic prepares a
secondary at $350B; a16z closes a $1.7B AI infrastructure fund; French police raid X's Paris
office over Grok.

**14 February:** Anthropic closes a **$30B Series G at $380B** — described as the largest AI
round to date — on annualized revenue above $14B, while xAI restructures after co-founder
departures despite a $1.25T valuation, and Mistral commits $1.4B to European expansion.

The analyst take attached to that brief is the sharpest judgment in the run: capital
deployment has outrun deployment capability, the market is separating into a mega-funded
foundation tier and a specialized application tier, and the open question is whether
sustainable business models emerge from current burn rates.

### March–April 2026 — carried by the forwards

Once original output stops, the April forwards carry the updates. They are worth reading as
the factual close of the record: OpenAI raises **$122B at a $830B valuation**; SpaceX files
confidentially for IPO and Nasdaq moves to speed its index entry; Anthropic discusses a Q4
IPO while preparing "Claude Mythos" and wins an injunction blocking a Pentagon blacklisting;
Google negotiates financing for a multibillion-dollar Anthropic data center; Bezos raises
$100B for AI manufacturing; Blackstone extends a data-center buying spree; Meta hires three
OpenAI Stargate leaders.

The most important forward is the last. The 12 April Briefing documents a renewed
enterprise-software selloff — ServiceNow and Snowflake down roughly 8% in a day, Figma down
half its value on the year — after UBS reported that over half its enterprise customer calls
now include anecdotes of containing non-AI software spend. That is the December *Trust Gap*
and *Constraint Economy* theses arriving as a price event.

## Page 3 — Trends across the run

### Trend 1 — Volume collapsed while quality rose, then both fell

Send frequency describes an arc, not a decline. The pipeline began as a firehose of
regenerations — fifteen sends on 4 January, twelve on 6 January, with the same report
reissued three to six times as the prompt was tuned — then consolidated into a single
well-formed daily brief, then thinned to three weekly products, then stopped.

The peak of output volume (early January) and the peak of output quality (mid-February) are
five weeks apart. Consolidating twenty-one franchises down to one daily brief is what
produced the best work in the archive.

### Trend 2 — Two pipelines diverged, and only one tracked reality

This is the most consequential pattern in the record. The products fall into two families
with different data sources, and they progressively contradicted each other.

The **news-grounded** family — AI Intelligence Brief, Morning Alpha, AI Analyst Morning Brief
— pulled from live sources and stayed accurate. The **entity-database** family — Command
Center, Strategic Brief, Master Blueprint, AI 7 Deep Dive, Dossier — read from a company
table that never refreshed. By March that table was wrong by an order of magnitude on nearly
every name.

| Company | Entity-DB product | News-grounded / verified | Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anthropic | $1.5B (Blueprint, 27 Mar) | $380B (Intel Brief, 14 Feb) | 253× |
| Anthropic | $5B (AI 7, 27 Mar) | $380B | 76× |
| OpenAI | $90B (AI 7, 27 Mar) | $830B (forward, 1 Apr) | 9.2× |
| xAI | $1B (Blueprint, 27 Mar) | $1.25T (Intel Brief, 14 Feb) | 1,250× |
| Databricks | $38–43B (Dossier & Blueprint) | $134B (Alpha, 25 Dec) | 3.1× |
| Safe Superintelligence | $80M–$200M | Target set at $43.2B | Incoherent |

The failure mode is visible in the templates themselves, not just the numbers. On the
6 January Strategic Brief, fourteen different companies — Nvidia, Google, Meta, OpenAI,
Perplexity, Glean and others — all carry the identical valuation `$157B (Oct 2024)`, a
fallback value substituting for a lookup miss. Every one of the twenty-six companies scores
exactly `85/100` on moat. On the 27 March AI 7, five of seven names show a target valuation
of `$0.0M`, all seven show `+35%` upside, all seven show a 90-day momentum delta of `+5`,
and every compute and talent signal reads `N/A` — while all seven are rated Buy.

> **The reader-facing consequence.** A recipient of the 27 March package received, in the
> same hour, a Blueprint valuing Anthropic at $1.5 billion and a conviction note valuing it
> at $5 billion — six weeks after the same account had correctly reported a $380 billion
> round. Uniform ratings with uniform upside are not analysis; they are a template rendering
> with no data behind it.

### Trend 3 — The model benchmark froze and was never noticed

The /// BENCHMARK product shipped daily from 25 December to 2 January with byte-identical
headline results every time: best value Llama 3.1 70B at `$0.00/1M`, smartest OpenAI o1 at
`1800`, best coder Llama 3.1 70B at `0% pass`. All fifteen models score `0%` on SWE-bench —
the metric was never wired up. Two models carry impossible Elo ratings (`193` and `127`).
The roster is frozen at mid-2024 while the same account's own briefs were covering GPT-5.2
and Claude Opus 4.6.

### Trend 4 — A consistent, diagnosable failure signature

- **Rendering** — eight sends delivered only the literal string `HTML Required`; several
  leaked raw ` ```html ` fences into the body; one on 3 January transmitted character-spaced
  markup (`< h 1 > D a i l y`), an encoding fault.
- **Runtime** — three `☠️ SYSTEM CRASHED` alerts on 14 December: an Airtable
  `422 Unprocessable Entity` on the `Weekly Briefs` table, then
  `'NoneType' object is not subscriptable` twice. Separately,
  `<lambda>() missing 2 required positional arguments`.
- **Placeholders in production** — the 17 December Morning Note shipped a full heatmap of
  "Company A" through "Company M" and three company cards for "Company X/Y/Z" to a work
  address.
- **Date hallucination** — two January sends are internally headed "October 23, 2023" and
  "October 2023" despite January datelines.
- **No version discipline** — engine stamps run in parallel and disagree: v6.0 and v11.0 on
  the same day, alongside SYSTEM V20.0, VOL 19.0, BENCHMARK V7 and MASTER ANALYST ENGINE V4.

### Trend 5 — Branding drifted toward attribution risk, then corrected

On 4 January at 20:06 and 20:12 the pipeline sent two reports titled **"GS RESEARCH"** with
bodies headed `GOLDMAN SACHS & CO. LLC` — a real firm's masthead on machine-generated
research, delivered to a work address. It was corrected within about forty minutes to
"Harrison's Advanced AI Analysis." Similarly, several products carried a `PitchBook
Intelligence` or `PitchBook Private Market Coverage` masthead with `CONFIDENTIAL` markings.
By 27 March an explicit disclosure had been added — that the report is automated, for
demonstration, with private-company estimates derived from news analysis. The direction of
travel is right; the disclosure arrived roughly twelve weeks after the branding did.

### Assessment — what the run demonstrates

The archive contains one genuinely good product and a lot of scaffolding around it. The AI
Intelligence Brief worked because it did one thing, ran once a day, and read from live
sources; its 14 February analyst take called the separation between funded-foundation and
specialized-application tiers before the April software selloff made it a price event.
Everything built on the static entity table failed the same way — plausible prose wrapped
around numbers that were never fetched, with uniform scores standing in for judgment.

Three things would fix the failing half without touching the working half: fail loudly
instead of substituting fallback values, so a lookup miss blocks the send rather than
printing `$157B` for fourteen companies; drop any field the pipeline cannot source rather
than rendering `$0.0M` or `N/A`; and stop deriving ratings from a template — a uniform `+35%`
across seven names is a tell that no valuation work occurred. The final shift to
hand-forwarding *The Information* suggests that judgment was already reached; the archive
supports it.
