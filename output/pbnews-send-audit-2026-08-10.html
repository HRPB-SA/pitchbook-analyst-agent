01

What the account actually is, and what it sent

The account is not a correspondent. It is the outbound endpoint of a self-built research bot, plus a small amount of human traffic. Almost every message is machine-generated equity and private-market research addressed to harrison.rolfes@pitchbook.com. Two exceptions exist: a run of thirteen newsletter forwards to hypdventures@gmail.com in April, and a personal brokerage-application exchange with Tradier on 11 March.

The run divides cleanly into seven phases. Each is defined by which report franchises were live, not by calendar boundaries — the pipeline was rebuilt repeatedly, and each rebuild replaced the previous product set rather than adding to it.

02

The updates: what the reports actually reported

Read in sequence, the outbound record tells a coherent story about the AI capital cycle over four months — accelerating megarounds through December and January, a February peak, and the first credible cracks appearing in April.

Dec 2025The megaround era opens

The December reports lead repeatedly on Databricks raising $4B at a $134B valuation on a $4.8B revenue run-rate, growing above 55% year over year. Around it: Anthropic's $13B Series F at $183B; Nvidia's acquisition of Groq for $20B, its largest on record; Anysphere (Cursor) at $9B; and a long tail of mid-size rounds — d-Matrix $275M at $2B, EliseAI $250M, Loadsmart $200M, MoEngage $180M, Terabase $130M, OpenAI-backed Chai Discovery $130M, Odeko $126M. On the product side the briefs track OpenAI's GPT-5.2 and GPT-5.2-Codex launches, the Disney–Sora licensing agreement, and developer app submissions opening in ChatGPT.

The December analysis frames all of this through a single thesis — The Constraint Economy — arguing that AI's trajectory is set by seven bottlenecks: compute, data, talent, distribution, regulation, trust and capital. A companion theme, The Trust Gap, argues capital-trust erosion is the binding risk and flags SaaS security credibility as the transmission channel. That call reads well four months later.

Jan 2026Capital mobilization, then whiplash

The daily briefs from 21 January onward carry an explicit disclosed-funding total, and the series is violently uneven: $40B on the 21st, $70B on the 22nd, three consecutive near-zero days over the 23rd–25th, $21.8B on the 26th, then $126B on the 29th and above $100B on the 30th. The briefs read this as capital concentration rather than breadth — fewer, larger, more strategic rounds, increasingly led by corporates rather than venture funds.

Feb 2026The peak

Two February briefs carry the largest items in the entire record. On 5 February: SpaceX acquires xAI in an AI–aerospace merger built around space-based compute; Nvidia's $20B investment into OpenAI nears completion; Anthropic prepares a secondary at $350B; a16z closes a $1.7B AI infrastructure fund; French police raid X's Paris office over Grok. On 14 February: Anthropic closes a $30B Series G at $380B — described as the largest AI round to date — on annualized revenue above $14B, while xAI restructures after co-founder departures despite a $1.25T valuation, and Mistral commits $1.4B to European expansion.

The analyst take attached to that brief is the sharpest judgment in the run: capital deployment has outrun deployment capability, the market is separating into a mega-funded foundation tier and a specialized application tier, and the open question is whether sustainable business models emerge from current burn rates.

Mar–Apr 2026Carried by the forwards

Once original output stops, the April forwards carry the updates. They are worth reading as the factual close of the record: OpenAI raises $122B at a $830B valuation; SpaceX files confidentially for IPO and Nasdaq moves to speed its index entry; Anthropic discusses a Q4 IPO while preparing "Claude Mythos" and wins an injunction blocking a Pentagon blacklisting; Google negotiates financing for a multibillion-dollar Anthropic data center; Bezos raises $100B for AI manufacturing; Blackstone extends a data-center buying spree; and Meta hires three OpenAI Stargate leaders.

The most important forward is the last. The 12 April Briefing documents a renewed enterprise-software selloff — ServiceNow and Snowflake down roughly 8% in a day, Figma down half its value on the year — after UBS reported that over half its enterprise customer calls now include anecdotes of containing non-AI software spend. That is the December Trust Gap and Constraint Economy theses arriving as a price event.

Trends across the run

Trend 1Volume collapsed while quality rose, then both fell

Send frequency describes an arc, not a decline. The pipeline began as a firehose of regenerations — fifteen sends on 4 January, twelve on 6 January, with the same report reissued three to six times as the prompt was tuned — then consolidated into a single well-formed daily brief, then thinned to three weekly products, then stopped.

The peak of output volume (early January) and the peak of output quality (mid-February) are five weeks apart. Consolidating twenty-one franchises down to one daily brief is what produced the best work in the archive.

Trend 2Two pipelines diverged — and only one tracked reality

This is the most consequential pattern in the record. The products fall into two families with different data sources, and they progressively contradicted each other.

The news-grounded family — AI Intelligence Brief, Morning Alpha, AI Analyst Morning Brief — pulled from live sources and stayed accurate. The entity-database family — Command Center, Strategic Brief, Master Blueprint, AI 7 Deep Dive, Dossier — read from a company table that never refreshed. By March that table was wrong by an order of magnitude on nearly every name.

The failure mode is visible in the templates themselves, not just the numbers. On the 6 January Strategic Brief, fourteen different companies — Nvidia, Google, Meta, OpenAI, Perplexity, Glean and others — all carry the identical valuation $157B (Oct 2024), a fallback value substituting for a lookup miss. Every one of the twenty-six companies scores exactly 85/100 on moat. On the 27 March AI 7, five of seven names show a target valuation of $0.0M, all seven show +35% upside, all seven show a 90-day momentum delta of +5, and every compute and talent signal reads N/A — while all seven are rated Buy.

The reader-facing consequence

A recipient of the 27 March package received, in the same hour, a Blueprint valuing Anthropic at $1.5 billion and a conviction note valuing it at $5 billion — six weeks after the same account had correctly reported a $380 billion round. Uniform ratings with uniform upside are not analysis; they are a template rendering with no data behind it.

Trend 3The model benchmark froze and was never noticed

The /// BENCHMARK product shipped daily from 25 December to 2 January with byte-identical headline results every time: best value Llama 3.1 70B at $0.00/1M, smartest OpenAI o1 at 1800, best coder Llama 3.1 70B at 0% pass. All fifteen models score 0% on SWE-bench — the metric was never wired up. Two models carry impossible Elo ratings (193 and 127). The roster is frozen at mid-2024 while the same account's own briefs were covering GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.6.

Trend 4A consistent, diagnosable failure signature

  • Rendering — eight sends delivered only the literal string HTML Required; several leaked raw ```html fences into the body; one on 3 January transmitted character-spaced markup (< h 1 > D a i l y), an encoding fault.
  • Runtime — three ☠️ SYSTEM CRASHED alerts on 14 December: an Airtable 422 Unprocessable Entity on the Weekly Briefs table, then 'NoneType' object is not subscriptable twice. Separately, <lambda>() missing 2 required positional arguments.
  • Placeholders in production — the 17 December Morning Note shipped a full heatmap of "Company A" through "Company M" and three company cards for "Company X/Y/Z" to a work address.
  • Date hallucination — two January sends are internally headed "October 23, 2023" and "October 2023" despite January datelines.
  • No version discipline — engine stamps run in parallel and disagree: v6.0 and v11.0 on the same day, alongside SYSTEM V20.0, VOL 19.0, BENCHMARK V7 and MASTER ANALYST ENGINE V4.

Trend 5Branding drifted toward attribution risk, then corrected

On 4 January at 20:06 and 20:12 the pipeline sent two reports titled "GS RESEARCH" with bodies headed GOLDMAN SACHS & CO. LLC — a real firm's masthead on machine-generated research, delivered to a work address. It was corrected within about forty minutes to "Harrison's Advanced AI Analysis." Similarly, several products carried a PitchBook Intelligence or PitchBook Private Market Coverage masthead with CONFIDENTIAL markings. By 27 March an explicit disclosure had been added — that the report is automated, for demonstration, with private-company estimates derived from news analysis. The direction of travel is right; the disclosure arrived roughly twelve weeks after the branding did.

AssessmentWhat the run demonstrates

The archive contains one genuinely good product and a lot of scaffolding around it. The AI Intelligence Brief worked because it did one thing, ran once a day, and read from live sources; its 14 February analyst take called the separation between funded-foundation and specialized-application tiers before the April software selloff made it a price event. Everything built on the static entity table failed the same way — plausible prose wrapped around numbers that were never fetched, with uniform scores standing in for judgment.

Three things would fix the failing half without touching the working half: fail loudly instead of substituting fallback values, so a lookup miss blocks the send rather than printing $157B for fourteen companies; drop any field the pipeline cannot source rather than rendering $0.0M or N/A; and stop deriving ratings from a template — a uniform +35% across seven names is a tell that no valuation work occurred. The final shift to hand-forwarding The Information suggests that judgment was already reached; the archive supports it.
