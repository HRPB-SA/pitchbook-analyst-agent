## (a) Overview

Cursor is an AI-native, VS Code-derived code editor built by Anysphere, founded in 2022 by four MIT students —
Michael Truell, Sualeh Asif, Arvid Lunnemark, and Aman Sanger. It launched in public beta in early 2023, raised
a $8M OpenAI Startup Fund-led seed that October, and compounded through a clean, fully-itemized funding ladder
(Series A $60M @ $400M in Aug 2024; Series B $100M @ $2.6B in Jan 2025; Series C $900M @ $9B in May 2025 —
summing to $1.068B raised) before the funding picture becomes considerably murkier from a reported Series D
onward (see (d)). NEXUS/PitchBook coverage tags it "T2-AI Leaders" / "Tier 2 — Challengers" in the AI-APP
sector. By the NEXUS financial-modeling team's own account, Cursor is **"the most capital-efficient frontier
AI company"** in its coverage universe: roughly $500M ARR against $1.068B raised (0.47x) and $5-6.7M revenue
per employee, versus $8M for Anthropic, $6.25M for OpenAI, and $2.16M for Databricks (all cited at the same
vintage). It reached ~360,000 paying developers and roughly 54% coding-AI market share by March 2025, and its
ARR trajectory from there is one of the steepest in this entire coverage universe — $20M (mid-2024) → ~$100M
(early 2025) → $500M (mid-2025) → ~$1B (late 2025) → ~$2B (March 2026) → $3-4B (mid-2026, contested — see (i)).

The defining, unresolved fact for this profile is corporate-control status. Cursor's parent, Anysphere, is
reported throughout 2026 to be the subject of a **$60B SpaceX acquisition option** (alternatively structured
as a $10B partnership) — the single most heavily covered storyline in this bundle, spanning **70 separate
timeline rows tagged M&A, none of them independently verified**. Coverage uses inconsistent tenses across the
year: "nearing" a deal at a $60B option price as of a June 11, 2026 digest; flat past-tense "SpaceX Acquires
Cursor" language in several June 2026 headlines; the legacy company record (extracted 2026-08-10) states it
plainly as completed ("Acquired by SpaceX for approximately $60 billion in June 2026"); and the most recent
dated item anywhere in this timeline, from 2026-08-01, still reads "**$60B Cursor Deal Nears Close**" — i.e.,
not yet closed, ten days before this profile's generation date. This system's own canon dossier explicitly
declines to canonize the number: *"SpaceX-Cursor $60B option is event-referenced only, not a canon figure."*
This profile follows that lead and does not assert a closed-deal status. Separately, Cursor drew a genuine
model-provenance controversy in March 2026 — its "Composer 2" model, marketed as proprietary and in-house, was
revealed within days to be built on Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.5 (a third-party Chinese open-weight model) — which
cost it at least one named enterprise customer (Notion) and sits alongside a separate, oddly-earlier-dated
congressional inquiry into Cursor's use of Chinese AI.
