## 🟢 PURSUE
### Hadrian Automation
**Date added:** June 15, 2026 \| **Source:** §12 Spotlight + §29 \| **Vertical:** Defense-Industrial / Space Manufacturing
**HQ:** Los Angeles, CA \| **Founded:** 2020
**What they do:** Hadrian builds software-defined precision-manufacturing factories that produce aerospace and defense components — the brackets, housings, and structural parts inside rockets, satellites, and weapons systems. Their model is factories-as-API: a customer uploads a CAD file, Hadrian's software programs the CNC machines, and the part ships. They are replacing an aging network of job shops whose owners are retiring with no successors, at a moment when US defense and space production has no domestic alternative at scale.
**Financing stage:** Series B \| **Total raised:** \~\$130M \| **Last round lead:** Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), with Lux Capital and Founders Fund
**Most likely acquirer:** Lockheed Martin or Northrop Grumman — both under congressional pressure to onshore precision-manufacturing supply chains, neither has a software-native machining capability. Lockheed's F-35 production bottlenecks on exactly this part class. Secondary acquirer: SpaceX, as a vertical-integration play now that SPCX is public and AI-segment capex (\$7.7B Q1) demands a controlled supply chain.
**VC most likely to lead next round:** a16z (already in), Lux Capital (deep-tech manufacturing thesis), Khosla Ventures (atoms-and-bits industrial). At Series C: Tiger Global or General Catalyst if they want growth-rate exposure to defense demand.
**The pitch:** The US defense industrial base is the most structurally undersupplied it has been since WWII. The Ukraine conflict exposed that the US can't produce 155mm shells at scale — precision machining is the same problem one level deeper. Hadrian is the only venture-scale company building the software to fix it. TAM is \$50B+ in addressable precision-manufacturing, growing with every new weapons system. The moat compounds: every part they make trains their process-data model, making the next part cheaper and faster. The SPCX IPO just made "space industrial base" a public-market narrative, pricing in years of launch demand that all runs through components Hadrian makes.
**Material risk:** Prime contractors (Lockheed, RTX, Boeing) could build captive machining capacity rather than buy from a vendor — vertical integration is the standard playbook when a supply chain becomes strategic. If one prime builds in-house, Hadrian loses its largest potential customer and most likely acquirer simultaneously.
**Verdict: PURSUE at Series B or earlier.** Post-SPCX, the demand arithmetic is public and the valuation will reprice at Series C. The window for a fund-fit check size (\$250K–\$1M) is closing.

### Eqlabs (EQ)
**Date added:** June 15, 2026 \| **Source:** §29 \| **Vertical:** Fintech / Equity Compensation
**HQ:** San Francisco, CA \| **Founded:** 2022
**What they do:** EQ builds AI-powered equity compensation management software for late-stage private companies and pre-IPO filers. Their platform automates 409A valuations, cap table modeling, secondary transaction compliance, and employee equity education — replacing the spreadsheet-and-law-firm workflow that breaks down exactly at the moment a company is most stressed (Series D+, tender offers, IPO prep).
**Financing stage:** Series A \| **Total raised:** \~\$18M \| **Last round lead:** Andreessen Horowitz (fintech fund)
**Most likely acquirer:** Carta (strategic, acqui-hire or feature absorption), Morgan Stanley (institutional equity services expansion), or Fidelity (private markets platform buildout).
**VC most likely to lead next round:** a16z fintech (already in), Ribbit Capital, or QED Investors — all have enterprise fintech theses and pre-IPO workflow exposure.
**The pitch:** The 2026 IPO pipeline (Anthropic, OpenAI, Databricks all filing this year) creates a 12-month surge in their exact customer profile — late-stage companies scrambling to clean up cap tables, run 409A valuations, and manage tender offers. EQ's timing is structural, not coincidental. Equity data is among the stickiest enterprise data; switching costs are high once a company's cap table lives in the platform. Who pays: CFOs and general counsel at companies with 500–5,000 employees and \$50M+ in equity outstanding.
**Material risk:** Carta is the incumbent with \$7.4B in equity under management. EQ needs a defensible wedge into Carta accounts or a meaningfully different buyer profile to avoid being acqui-hired before they reach escape velocity.
**Verdict: PURSUE.** The IPO wave is a 12-month tailwind that directly benefits their customer base. First conversation before Series B closes.

### Archetype AI
**Date added:** June 15, 2026 \| **Source:** §29 \| **Vertical:** Industrial AI / IoT
**HQ:** San Francisco, CA \| **Founded:** 2023
**What they do:** Archetype builds foundation models for physical-world sensor data — specifically, industrial IoT streams (vibration, temperature, acoustic, power) that traditional LLMs cannot interpret. Their model ingests raw sensor feeds from factories, data centers, and energy infrastructure and outputs predictive maintenance alerts, anomaly detection, and operational optimization recommendations without requiring custom ML engineering from the customer.
**Financing stage:** Seed \| **Total raised:** \~\$12M \| **Last round lead:** Lux Capital
**Most likely acquirer:** Siemens (industrial automation), GE Vernova (energy infrastructure AI), or a hyperscaler (Microsoft/Azure, Google Cloud) for data center thermal/power monitoring.
**VC most likely to lead next round:** Lux Capital (already in, deep-tech manufacturing thesis), Breakthrough Energy Ventures (energy-infrastructure angle), or Andreessen Horowitz (infra AI play).
**The pitch:** The compute buildout driving AI data center construction creates a massive new customer base that didn't exist 18 months ago — data center operators need exactly this for thermal and power monitoring. Archetype's moat is proprietary training data from industrial partners (not replicable quickly) and the model architecture for time-series physical-world data (genuinely different from LLM architecture). Who pays: industrial operators, data center managers, energy companies. The AI data center angle is a near-term revenue catalyst independent of the longer industrial sales cycle.
**Material risk:** Industrial sales cycles are 12–18 months and the company is 2 years old — cash efficiency is the critical question at this stage. A seed round of \$12M may not be enough runway to reach the first enterprise contracts.
**Verdict: PURSUE.** Seed stage, capital efficient, and the data center angle is a near-term revenue catalyst. Check size (\$250K) fits perfectly at this stage.

## 🟡 WATCH
### Etched
**Date added:** June 15, 2026 \| **Source:** §29 Contrarian Pick \| **Vertical:** AI Silicon / ASIC
**HQ:** San Francisco, CA \| **Founded:** 2022
**What they do:** Etched builds ASICs (application-specific integrated circuits) hardwired exclusively to run Transformer architecture neural networks — they burn the architecture into silicon rather than running it on programmable GPUs. The result is 10–1,000x energy efficiency improvement over NVIDIA GPUs for inference workloads on Transformer models.
**Financing stage:** Series A \| **Total raised:** \~\$120M \| **Last round leads:** Primary Venture Partners and Positive Sum
**Most likely acquirer:** AWS (custom silicon strategy, already runs Trainium/Inferentia), Microsoft Azure (inference cost reduction), or Meta (LLaMA inference at scale).
**VC most likely to lead next round:** NVentures (NVIDIA strategic — defensive investment), Andreessen Horowitz (semiconductors thesis), or a sovereign wealth fund seeking chip independence.
**The pitch (variant perception):** Most investors pass because hardwired-Transformer chips bet on architecture permanence. The variant perception: every major frontier lab (Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI) is publicly committed to inference at scale, the Transformer architecture has survived every "replacement" cycle for 4+ years, and NVIDIA's gross margins (\~75%) create a structural incentive for alternatives. ASIC lead time is 18–24 months, meaning a competitor filing today doesn't tape out until late 2027. AI inference silicon is a \$50B+ market by 2030 at current buildout trajectories.
**Material risk:** A post-Transformer architecture (Mamba, SSM variants, or something not yet public) makes the chip obsolete. This is a binary architecture bet — if Transformers fade, the moat evaporates entirely.
**Verdict: WATCH.** Thesis risk is real (architecture obsolescence), but moat duration is longer than most investors price. Revisit at Series B if Transformer dominance holds through 2027.

## 🔭 FRONTIER PICKS (Markets That Don't Exist Yet)
### Sovereign AI Attestation Infrastructure
**Date added:** June 15, 2026 \| **Thesis trigger:** Anthropic Fable 5 recall, June 12, 2026
**The thesis:** The market forms when the first post-IPO AI incident with public shareholders forces mandatory capability certification. The Fable 5 recall is the founding event — a government just demonstrated it can recall a model citing an undisclosed jailbreak, with no technical audit, no public documentation, and no compliance pathway for the vendor. The company that builds the auditable attestation layer — what a model can and cannot do, verified by an independent third party — becomes the PCAOB of AI. The positioned company looks today like an evals startup with government contract ambitions and boring revenue.
**Inflection event:** First Congressional hearing that demands one following an AI incident at a public company. Estimated: 12–24 months.
**What to look for now:** Evals startups with government or insurance customers, not just lab partnerships. Companies working on AI red-teaming that are building methodology, not just tooling.
