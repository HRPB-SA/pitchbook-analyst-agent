## 🎓 12. CFA PREP — Level I · Session 1 of 10 · Ethical & Professional Standards
**Domain:** Ethical & Professional Standards (15% exam weight — highest return per study hour at the margin)
**Concept: Standard II(A) — Material Nonpublic Information (MNPI)**
MNPI = information that is (1) not yet public AND (2) material (a reasonable investor would consider it significant). Trading on MNPI violates Standard II(A) regardless of whether the issuer is public or private. **Mosaic theory exception:** combining legitimately obtained nonpublic information with public data to reach a nonpublic conclusion is protected, provided no single piece of nonpublic information is itself material.
**Practice Q:** Sarah, a private equity analyst, receives a call from an AI company founder who mentions their next funding round will be announced Monday at a valuation “well above” current secondary prices. Before the announcement, Sarah adjusts her fund’s secondary market position. Violation?
- A) No — private companies exempt from MNPI rules
- B) **Yes — valuation information is material and nonpublic**
- C) No — secondary market transactions don’t trigger MNPI
- D) Yes — but only if she profited
**Answer: B.** MNPI applies to private issuers. The pre-announcement valuation is material (secondary investors would consider it significant) and nonpublic. Options A, C, and D are common distractors — private status, transaction venue, and profit are all irrelevant to whether a violation occurred.
**Harrison connection:** Your Bloomberg, Business Insider, and Axios relationships mean you regularly receive early AI company intel. Combining public ARR disclosures + public benchmarks + AIBQ framework analysis = mosaic theory, fully protected. But the moment a source gives you a specific pre-announcement valuation, round size, or close date — that’s MNPI regardless of whether the company is public.
