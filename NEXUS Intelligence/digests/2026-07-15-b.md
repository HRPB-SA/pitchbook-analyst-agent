IBM shed **25%** on Jul 14 — from an all-time high above **\$330 in June** to **\~\$217** at Tuesday's close — after pre-announcing June-quarter revenue up only **1% to \$17.2B**, EPS down **2% to \$2.27**, mainframe-linked infrastructure revenue **−7% YoY** (vs a low-single-digit expected decline), and **software growth decelerating from 11% in Q1 to 5% in Q2** (T2, The Information/Laura Bratton; pre-announcement ahead of the Jul 22 print). The reframe: this is not "AI displaced IBM's product." It is the first **dollar-denominated, CEO-attested** evidence that AI-infrastructure component inflation is a **tax on the whole enterprise IT budget** — and the tax is being paid first by seat-and-license software, out of the same wallet. Microsoft's \~\$190B 2026 capex including \~\$25B of pure component-price inflation (T3) is the same fact from the buyer's side.
Second-order: \~20 hours later, at 10:00 ET on Jul 15, Anthropic, Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman launched **Ode with Anthropic** — a standalone enterprise AI services firm built on Fractional AI (acquired May 2026), led by Chris Taylor (CEO) and Eddie Siegel (CTO), backed by Goldman Sachs, General Atlantic, Leonard Green, Apollo, GIC and Sequoia, targeting mid-size financial-services, healthcare, retail, manufacturing and software buyers (T1/T2, Business Wire). The mechanism that matters is the **structure**: services revenue is headcount-linked and carries systems-integrator margins, not software margins. By capitalizing the delivery labor in a **separate company funded by private equity**, Anthropic captures the reallocated enterprise budget as Claude consumption at software margin while the labor-intensive half sits on someone else's balance sheet — three months before an October roadshow, on an S-1 whose margin arc (−94% → 40% → 63% 2027E → 77% 2028E) is the most contested line in the file. **30-day hook:** the most likely change is that a named systems integrator publicly reprices, hedges or exits its Claude practice, converting Ode from a distribution story into a channel-conflict story and putting a number on Anthropic's platform risk (§3).

### §23 — FINANCIAL CONCEPT OF THE DAY
**Term premium.** *Definition:* the extra yield demanded to hold a long bond instead of rolling short ones — compensation for duration risk, separate from expected policy rates. *Mechanism:* a long yield decomposes into the expected average short rate over the term **plus** term premium. If policy expectations fall (two soft prints) while the 30Y stays pinned at **5.102%** — above 2023's highs, near the highest since before the GFC — the difference is not the Fed. It is the market charging more to fund duration at all: supply, inflation variance, fiscal risk. *Live anchor:* this is the number the IPO window actually hangs on. Discount-rate relief for a long-duration equity comes from the **long** end, and the long end did not participate in yesterday's rally. A \~\$3.6T pipeline has to clear against a term premium two disinflation prints did not touch — a cleaner explanation for SPCX sitting at its offer than any sentiment story. **Usable tool:** for any IPO-window argument, ask which end of the curve it needs. ### §24 — CFA STUDY (Level I, Dec 2026)
**Domain:** Equity Investments *(rotation: Corp Fin → Equity).* **Concept:** justified forward P/E and the sustainable-growth trap.
**Vignette.** An analyst values a mature software firm using P₀/E₁ = (1 − b) / (r − g). ROE **12%**, retention **b = 0.60**, required return **r = 10%**. The analyst argues that because management guides to faster growth, g should be raised to **11%** with all other inputs held constant. The most appropriate criticism is that:
**A)** g of 11% exceeds ROE, inconsistent with b = 0.60 · **B)** the model produces a negative P/E, which is economically meaningless and signals the model is inapplicable · **C)** r should rise alongside g because faster growth implies higher risk · **D)** the payout ratio must be recalculated because g and b are jointly determined
*(Transition beat — answer below.)*
**Answer: B.** With r = 10% and g = 11%, r − g = −1%, so P₀/E₁ = 0.40 / (−0.01) = **−40x**. A negative justified P/E is the model announcing its own boundary condition: Gordon growth requires **g \< r**, in perpetuity, without exception. **Wrong-answer reasoning:** **A** is *true* but not the most appropriate criticism — sustainable g = b × ROE = 0.60 × 0.12 = **7.2%**, so 11% is unsupported; but that criticizes the *input's plausibility*, and the model still computes at 7.2%. At g = 11% it doesn't compute at all — an inapplicability defect outranks an implausibility defect. **C** is a real-world instinct the question doesn't ask about and Gordon growth doesn't model. **D** inverts causality: b is an input, g is derived. **Honesty ledger:** **conceptual**, not careless — A is what you pick if you're pattern-matching "sustainable growth" and haven't internalized that g ≥ r is a *domain* violation, not a *calibration* error. Cleared — mechanism articulated. *Days to exam: \~152.*
