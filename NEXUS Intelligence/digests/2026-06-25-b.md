## MODULE A — INTELLIGENCE CORE
### §1 OPENING BELL / LEAD SIGNAL
The Frontier Five story this morning runs through OpenAI, not Anthropic. OpenAI and Broadcom yesterday unveiled Jalapeño — OpenAI's first custom Intelligence Processor, an inference-only ASIC designed in a compressed 9-month cycle and targeting initial deployment by end-2026. \[T2, OpenAI blog + The Information, June 25.\] The chip is reticle-sized (\~840mm²), pairs with HBM3/4, and is aimed specifically at the high-volume, latency-sensitive inference workloads behind ChatGPT, Codex, and API products. Early lab samples are reportedly running GPT-5.3-Codex-Spark at production target frequency and power, with OpenAI claiming performance-per-watt that beats current state-of-the-art by a meaningful margin — though final public benchmarks remain pending, so the claim is T2 at best.
The analytical reframe: Jalapeño is a CI event first, a CE event second. On the CI side, owning inference silicon closes the most expensive part of the compute dependency on Nvidia — every GPT answer currently routes through a general-purpose GPU that costs more and wastes cycles on non-LLM workloads. On the CE side, if the chip ramps as planned, OpenAI's inference cost per token drops materially, which is the single largest lever on its path to positive gross margin. The caveat: deployment at scale is a 2027+ story; engineering samples running in a lab are not production infrastructure. The \$18B initial platform cost (The Information, T2) also means this is capital-intensive before it's capital-efficient.
Week ahead: the operative question is whether Microsoft confirms its role as a primary Jalapeño buyer — the blog post implied it without naming it. That confirmation would be the T1 event that locks CI implications into OpenAI's AIBQ score.

### §23 FINANCIAL CONCEPT OF THE DAY
**Concept: Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) vs. Return on Equity (ROE) for capital-intensive tech companies.**
**Plain definition:** ROIC measures how efficiently a company generates returns from all the capital it has deployed — debt and equity combined — in its operating assets. ROE measures returns to equity holders only and can be artificially inflated by leverage.
**Mechanism (why it matters):** For AI infrastructure companies, the distinction is critical. A company that finances data centers and chip programs with \$34.5B in chip-facility debt (Anthropic) or an \$18B chip platform (OpenAI) is deploying significant capital from both equity and debt sources. ROE would look excellent if the company generates any earnings at all on that leveraged base. ROIC tells you the true economic return — whether the infrastructure investments generate returns above the weighted average cost of capital (WACC). If ROIC \< WACC, the company is destroying value even while reporting revenue growth.
**Live anchor:** Jalapeño costs OpenAI's platform \~\$18B (T2). If we assume a 10-year useful life and a modest return on inference revenue, the implied ROIC on the chip program needs to exceed OpenAI's WACC (probably 8–12% for a pre-revenue infrastructure program) before it's value-creating. The 9-month design cycle is impressive; the capital efficiency of the chip program is unresolved until deployment and utilization data exist. This is a live input for the IPO model's CI/infrastructure tab.

### §24 CFA STUDY
**Domain: Ethics (Level I, December 2026 — active rotation)**
**Concept today: Standard VI(A) — Disclosure of Conflicts**
Standard VI(A) requires members to make full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair their independence and objectivity. Disclosure must be made to employers and clients/prospects. The standard doesn't prohibit conflicts — it requires transparency about them.
**Vignette:**
Maria Chen, CFA, covers five technology companies for a boutique research firm. One of the companies she covers, TechCo, recently awarded a consulting contract to her brother-in-law's firm. She does not disclose this relationship in her research reports or to her employer, believing it is too tangential to constitute a material conflict. Her employer discovers the relationship six months later.
Which of the following best describes Maria's situation?
A) Maria has violated Standard VI(A) because she failed to disclose a potential conflict that could reasonably be expected to impair her objectivity.
B) Maria has not violated any standard because the relationship is indirect (brother-in-law) and she did not personally benefit.
C) Maria has violated Standard I(C) (Misrepresentation) by omitting a material fact in her research.
D) Maria's employer has the primary obligation to disclose conflicts; her individual responsibility is secondary.
*\[Answer after transition:\]*
The answer is **A**. Standard VI(A) requires disclosure of conflicts that "could reasonably be expected to impair" objectivity — not just conflicts that actually did impair it. A familial connection to a company that benefits a covered company is a textbook example. The standard is forward-looking and precautionary. B is wrong because indirect benefit is still a conflict. C is tempting but misroutes to the wrong standard — VI(A) is specifically the conflict disclosure standard. D reverses the hierarchy — individuals bear primary responsibility for their own disclosures.
