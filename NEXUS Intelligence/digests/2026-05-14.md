**Session:** 7 — Standard III(A): Loyalty, Prudence, and Care
**Concept:** Standard III(A) requires that members place their clients' interests ahead of both their employer's interests and their own. The standard creates a strict hierarchy: client first, then employer, then self. The prudent investor test is the operative framework: would a reasonable professional managing the client's money make this decision? The most common exam trap is confusing "client" with "employer." Members owe fiduciary duty to ultimate beneficiaries — pensioners, fund investors, end clients — not to the entity signing their paycheck. An analyst or manager who follows an employer directive that damages clients violates III(A) even if the employer's instruction was explicit and commercially motivated.
**Practice Question:**
Marcus Webb is a portfolio manager at Crestline Asset Management. His firm has just agreed to a \$200 million strategic partnership with Apex Technology, which will generate significant fee revenue for Crestline. The CIO sends Webb a memo stating that Webb's accounts should "maintain meaningful exposure to Apex Technology's publicly traded affiliate, Apex Listed Corp., in recognition of the partnership." Apex Listed Corp. currently trades at a 40% premium to sector peers on a P/E basis. Webb is uncertain about the valuation but adds the position to three client portfolios.
Which of the following best describes Webb's obligation under Standard III(A)?
A) Webb may add the position if he discloses the partnership relationship to clients before executing
B) Webb must add the position because the CIO directive reflects employer interest, which overrides individual portfolio discretion
C) Webb violates III(A) by adding a security that does not pass the prudent investor test, regardless of the CIO directive
D) Webb is permitted to add the position if Apex Listed Corp. constitutes less than 5% of each affected portfolio
**Answer: C.**
Webb's duty under III(A) runs to his clients, not to Crestline or its commercial relationship with Apex. A 40% P/E premium to peers fails the prudent investor test without independent analytical justification. The CIO directive does not override fiduciary duty — employer instructions are not a defense under the Standards.
Option A fails because disclosure of a conflict does not eliminate the obligation to make prudent investment decisions; disclosure is required but not sufficient — it is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one.
Option B directly inverts the III(A) hierarchy. The exam tests this inversion specifically because it reflects a plausible real-world pressure that candidates might rationalize.
Option D introduces a percentage threshold that does not exist in the standard. Prudence is not defined by position sizing — a 3% position in a pump-and-dump scheme violates III(A) just as much as a 10% one.
**Harrison connection:** This standard maps directly to the \$100B Entry Fee report's WeWork IPO disclosure analog. A research analyst who hedges a negative finding because the subject company is a potential PitchBook client faces the same structure as Webb — commercial relationship pressure vs. duty to the end reader. The standard says the reader wins. Every AIBQ score published publicly is subject to III(A) in spirit, even outside the formal CFA context.
