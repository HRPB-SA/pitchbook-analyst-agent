## 🎓 13. CFA PREP
**Domain:** Ethics — Week 2 of 3
**Concept:** Standard I(B) — Independence and Objectivity. Members must use reasonable care to maintain independence and objectivity in professional activities. Key trap: gifts, travel, or entertainment from covered entities. The exam tests whether analysts can identify situations where *perceived* independence is compromised, even if actual bias is absent. Common formula: if a reasonable person would question your objectivity, the standard is violated.
**Practice Question:**
Sarah Chen, CFA, is a senior analyst covering cloud infrastructure companies. Her firm's asset management division holds a large position in Alphabet. Sarah's coverage universe includes Google Cloud, which she rates as "Overweight." Anthropic, a private company she follows, just announced a \$200B commitment to Google Cloud. A portfolio manager at her firm asks Sarah to write a research note highlighting how this commitment benefits Google Cloud's revenue outlook.
Which action best complies with Standard I(B)?
A) Write the note as requested, since the \$200B commitment is public information
B) Write the note but disclose her firm's Alphabet position in the note
C) Decline to write the note because her firm holds Alphabet
D) Write the note with objective analysis, disclose the firm's position, and ensure the note reflects her independent professional judgment
**Answer:** D. Standard I(B) does not prohibit covering companies where the firm has positions. It requires disclosure and independent judgment. A is wrong because public information doesn't eliminate the conflict from the firm's position. B is partially right but omits the independent judgment requirement. C is overly restrictive — disclosure + independence is the standard, not blanket recusal.

## 📜 19. THE ANALOG
Anthropic committing \$200B to Google Cloud while simultaneously signing SpaceX and expanding on AWS resembles **AT&T's multi-vendor fiber strategy in 1997–2000**, when AT&T signed massive capacity agreements with Corning, Lucent, and Nortel simultaneously — hedging against single-supplier dependency while locking capacity before competitors could. AT&T's bet worked for 3 years: it secured the best capacity at the lowest prices. Then demand collapsed in 2001, and the fixed commitments became stranded liabilities. The question that determines whether the analog holds: does AI compute demand grow fast enough to absorb \$200B over 5 years, or does efficiency gains (model distillation, inference optimization) reduce demand before the commitment is amortized? **The analog partially applies because the demand destruction risk is real — but AI compute demand is growing at 80x (E14), not the 2x that fiber demand grew in 1999.**

## 🔁 22. THE CONTRARIAN LENS
**The consensus:** Anthropic's SpaceX Colossus 1 deal proves it's winning the compute race — more capacity = more capability = higher valuation.
**The contrarian case:** Anthropic is over-committing to compute at precisely the moment efficiency gains are accelerating. Model distillation, speculative decoding, and inference optimization are reducing the compute needed per query by 30–50% annually. If Anthropic locks \$200B with Google + 300MW with SpaceX + \$30B with Azure + 5GW with Amazon, it's building for a demand curve that may flatten before these commitments mature. The \$80B+ aggregate infrastructure commitment could become the AI equivalent of fiber overbuilding in 2000.
**The tell:** Anthropic's blended cost-per-query drops \>40% in the next 12 months while total compute utilization stays below 70% across all providers.
**Conviction:** Low. The 80x demand growth (E14) makes demand destruction unlikely in the near term — but efficiency gains compound, and this is the right contrarian bet to track.
