When a buyer uses more, they pay more, which means the seller's revenue scales with the customer's success — the 
engine of high net revenue retention. The structural trade-off is that UBR makes revenue less predictable than annual recurring commitments because customer consumption can be lumpy, seasonal, or discretionary. High-gross-margin products with low marginal cost of delivery (like software API calls) handle UBR well because the incremental cost of a customer using more is near-zero; the seller captures most of the upside.<br><br>\*\*Live anchor:\*\* Today's Databricks Genie PAYG transition is a direct application. Databricks is shifting Genie Spaces, Genie Code, and Genie One from whatever prior billing structure to UBR. The analytical tool this concept provides: to evaluate this move, ask (1) what is the marginal cost of a Genie session (near zero if it's LLM inference on existing infrastructure), (2) what was the prior revenue per user under the old model, and (3) at what consumption level does a PAYG user generate more revenue than a seat-licensed user would have? If that crossover point is achievable at realistic usage rates, UBR is accretive. If most users consume below the crossover, it destroys ARR.<br><br>\*Boundary note: §24 owns curriculum mechanics; UBR as a CFA concept routes there if it intersects Level I Corporate Finance or Equity Valuation reading.\*<br><br>---<br><br>### §24 CFA STUDY<br><br>\*\*Domain rotation: Economics (Econ) — Pricing and Market Structures\*\*<br><br>\*\*Concept:\*\* Anchoring to today's lead event — price discrimination and usage-based pricing mechanics within competitive market structures.<br><br>\*\*Concept:\*\* Second-degree price discrimination.
