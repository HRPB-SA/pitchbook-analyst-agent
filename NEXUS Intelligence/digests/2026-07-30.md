Flexing up means demand was insufficient at the original spread — investors required higher compensation to clear the book.<br><br>\*\*Mechanism (the why):\*\* Lev
eraged loans are sold to institutional investors (CLOs, loan funds, hedge funds) who assess credit risk relative to the loan's spread over SOFR or a reference rate. When a loan's underlying credit story weakens — either because the borrower's fundamentals deteriorate or because macro risk-off sentiment reduces appetite for the asset class — investors demand a higher spread to compensate for the additional perceived risk. Underwriters who have committed to place the loan must either flex up (increase the yield), syndicate a smaller amount, or hold the risk on their own balance sheet. Flexing up is the path of least institutional friction but signals market conditions publicly.<br><br>\*\*Live anchor:\*\* CoreWeave flexed up the yield on its \$2.6B leveraged loan tied to Anthropic compute capacity \[T2: Bloomberg, 2026-07-29\] because investors were "increasingly cautious about AI-related debt amid mounting bubble concerns." sm:\*\* Modified duration measures the approximate percentage change in a bond's price for a 1% (100 bps) change in yield, with the sign inverted (yields up → price down). The formula: %ΔPrice ≈ −ModDuration × ΔYield. So: −4.45 × 0.005 (50 bps = 0.005 in decimal) = −0.02225, or −2.225%. Answer A is wrong because the sign is incorrect — rising yields reduce prices, not increase them. Answer C is wrong because it uses the full 100 bps (1%) change, not 50 bps. Answer D is wrong on both sign and magnitude.<br><br>\*\*Wrong-answer reasoning:\*\* C is the most common careless error — students confuse the definition of modified duration (sensitivity per 100 bps) with the actual yield change given in the problem.
