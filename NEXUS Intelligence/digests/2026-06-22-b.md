### §23 FINANCIAL CONCEPT OF THE DAY
**Deemed Export Doctrine (15 CFR 734.13)**
Plain definition: A "deemed export" is a release of controlled technology or source code to a foreign national inside the United States. The "deemed" terminology reflects that the law treats the domestic disclosure as if the item had been physically exported — triggering the same export licensing requirements as a physical shipment abroad.
Mechanism (the why): U.S. export control law was designed to prevent sensitive technology from reaching foreign governments or adversaries. But technology is often transferred through knowledge, not physical goods — a foreign national engineer reading source code, handling controlled algorithms, or operating controlled systems inside the US is functionally equivalent to exporting that knowledge. The "deemed export" rule closes this gap.
Live anchor: Anthropic received a Commerce Department letter invoking deemed export authority at 5:21 PM ET on June 12. Because Anthropic could not verify user nationality in real time across hundreds of millions of API requests, it faced a binary choice: implement blanket global suspension or risk willful non-compliance. It chose blanket suspension. The usable analytical tool: when evaluating AI companies for IPO, deemed export risk is a new category of regulatory risk that was not priced in frontier AI valuations before June 12. It belongs in S-1 risk factors and in AIBQ GO sub-scores going forward.
