A monopsonist faces an upward-sloping labor supply curve: to hire more workers, it must raise wages for all current workers (not just the marginal hire). This makes the marginal cost of labor \*higher\* than the wage rate, so the monopsonist optimally employs fewer workers at a lower wage than the competitive equilibrium — a deadweight loss to efficiency, a transfer from workers to the employer.<br><br>\*\*Live anchor:\*\* Kevin Weil's move to the Stoke Space board (July 8, §3) raises a micro-version of this dynamic: AI executive talent operates in a thin market where a handful of frontier labs are the dominant buyers.
