## 🎓 12. CFA PREP — Level I · Session 1 of 10 · Ethical & Professional Standards
**Domain:** Ethical & Professional Standards (15% exam weight — highest return per study hour at the margin)
**Concept: Standard II(A) — Material Nonpublic Information (MNPI)**
An analyst possesses MNPI when information is: (1) not yet available to the public, and (2) something a reasonable investor would consider significant. Trading on MNPI or inducing others to trade violates Standard II(A) regardless of whether the security is publicly or privately held. The **mosaic theory** exception: an analyst may reach a nonpublic conclusion by combining legitimately obtained nonpublic information with public data, provided no single piece of nonpublic information is itself material.
Formula-equivalent for MNPI test: **MNPI = Nonpublic AND Material**. Both conditions must be true simultaneously. Public information about a private company (e.g., a press release about headcount) is not MNPI even if the company is not publicly traded.
**Practice Q:**
Sarah, a private equity analyst, receives a call from a founder of a private AI company who mentions their next funding round will be announced Monday at a valuation "well above" the current secondary market price. Before the announcement, Sarah adjusts her fund's secondary market position. Has Sarah violated Standard II(A)?
- A) No — private companies are exempt from MNPI rules
- B) **Yes — the valuation information is material and nonpublic**
- C) No — secondary market transactions don’t trigger MNPI obligations
- D) Yes — but only if she generates a profit
**Answer: B.** MNPI applies regardless of whether the issuer is public or private. The upcoming round valuation is material (a reasonable investor in the secondary market would consider it significant) and nonpublic (not yet released). The exemption for private companies does not exist in the CFA Standards. Option D is a common distractor — intent and profit are irrelevant to whether a violation occurred.
**Harrison connection:** Your media relationships with Bloomberg, Business Insider, and Axios mean you regularly receive early AI company intel. The test is two-part: (1) is it material, and (2) is it nonpublic at the time you receive it? Combining public ARR disclosures + public benchmark results + your own AIBQ framework analysis = mosaic theory, fully protected. But the moment a source gives you a specific pre-announcement valuation, round size, or close date — that’s MNPI regardless of whether Anthropic or OpenAI is public. This also applies to any HYPD deal flow discussions where a founder shares non-public terms.
