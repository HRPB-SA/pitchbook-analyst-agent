### §23 Financial Concept of the Day
**Vendor financing (and circular financing).** Definition: a supplier lends to — or guarantees the revenue of — its own customers so they can afford its product. Mechanism (the why): it de-risks the buyer's purchase and sustains the supplier's demand, but transfers credit and inventory risk onto the supplier's balance sheet. Live anchor: Nvidia's GPU backstop is the textbook case — a rent-back guarantee on unsold GPUs lets subprime chip buyers get loans; the analytical tool is to discount reported end-demand by the share that is vendor-financed, and to watch backstop terms as a late-cycle tell. (Overlaps §24 Corp-Fin: capital structure.)

### §24 CFA Study
**Domain: Ethics** (Level I, Dec 2026). Concept: Standard II(A), Material Nonpublic Information — apt given today's export/pre-clearance themes and the desk's own MNPI checkpoint.
*Vignette.* An analyst covering private AI labs learns from a vendor briefing (under no confidentiality agreement) that a lab's flagship model was pulled from a benchmark during an outage. She combines this with public benchmark archives and a public export notice to conclude the lab likely ceded cyber-share, then publishes a downgrade note. Which is most accurate?
- A. She violated II(A) because the outage detail was nonpublic.
- B. She violated II(A) because combining sources to reach a material conclusion is prohibited.
- C. She did not violate II(A); the mosaic theory permits material conclusions from public and nonmaterial-nonpublic pieces.
- D. She violated II(A) unless she obtained the issuer's consent to publish.
*(Transition — reason before the reveal.)*
**Answer: C.** The mosaic theory expressly permits an analyst to reach a material conclusion by assembling public information with nonmaterial-nonpublic details — that is analysis, not a violation. A is wrong because a single nonmaterial detail (an outage, itself widely reported) is not material nonpublic information. B misstates the standard — synthesis is the analyst's job. D imports a consent requirement that does not exist. **Honesty ledger:** if you picked A, that is a *conceptual* gap on the material-vs-nonmaterial line, not a careless slip — clears only when you can articulate that materiality turns on price-relevance, not on whether a fact was spoken privately.
