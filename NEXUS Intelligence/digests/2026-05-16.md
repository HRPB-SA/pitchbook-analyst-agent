## 🎓 13. CFA PREP
Domain: Ethical & Professional Standards (Week 4 of rotation, Session 8)
Concept: Standard III(B) — Fair Dealing
Fair Dealing requires members to deal fairly — not equally — with all clients when disseminating investment recommendations. "Fairly" means without favoritism, in a consistent systematic manner. The standard does not require simultaneous communication; it prohibits preferential early access to material, time-sensitive information regardless of fee tier. Exam trap: confusing "fair" with "equal."
Practice Question: Analyst Jordan Park sends a Databricks upgrade note (Hold to Buy) to the firm's largest fee-paying client two hours before distributing it to the broader client list. Park's rationale: large clients pay higher fees and receive research first. Under Standard III(B), Park has:
A) Not violated — different service tiers may receive research at different times
B) Violated — all clients must receive recommendations simultaneously
C) Violated — preferential early access to a material recommendation change constitutes unfair dealing regardless of fee tier
D) Not violated — the client list received the note the same business day
Answer: C. Selective early disclosure of a material recommendation change is preferential treatment regardless of fee tier. Distractor A fails: tiered service (more frequent reviews, direct contact) does not extend to sequenced material research access. Distractor B sets too high a bar — simultaneous distribution is best practice, not a mandate. Distractor D fails: "same business day" is irrelevant; two hours of exclusive access to a Buy recommendation is material and actionable.
Harrison connection: If AIBQ were licensed as a commercial research product, Standard III(B) would govern how revised AIBQ scores are distributed across paying subscribers. Any tiered distribution of a score change requires a written fair-dealing policy specifying the access ladder and maximum permissible lag.

## 📜 19. THE ANALOG
The Anthropic-SpaceXAI deal — a dominant competitor leasing stranded compute to a faster-growing rival, monetizing an asset its own products could not fill — has a precise analog: Lucent Technologies in 2001.
Lucent built the world's largest fiber optic network for the telecom boom. When the dot-com bust arrived, Lucent had hundreds of millions of dollars of installed fiber running at approximately 12% utilization. Rather than write it down, Lucent signed long-term dark fiber leases to competitors including Genuity and Sprint. Three years later, most leases were rewritten at deep discounts as counterparties filed for bankruptcy, and Lucent's infrastructure advantage was never restored.
The analog partially applies. The 11% utilization and co-founder departures mirror the Lucent dynamic on the supply side. The analog breaks at the demand side: Anthropic is growing at 1,400% YoY with \$30B ARR — not a dot-com company with vaporware revenue. What holds: the supply-chain vulnerability if Colossus 2 also fails to generate proprietary model traction, leaving xAI financially dependent on the Anthropic lease and Musk making increasingly arbitrary reclaim decisions as the SpaceX IPO narrative evolves.
