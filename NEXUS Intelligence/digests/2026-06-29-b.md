## §23 Financial Concept of the Day
**Duration / the discount-rate effect on long-duration equity.** Plain definition: the sensitivity of an asset's present value to a change in the discount rate — the further out the cash flows, the larger the price move per basis point. Mechanism (the why): PV = ΣCF/(1+r)\^t; raising r shrinks distant CFs geometrically, so a company whose value is mostly terminal/long-dated (a pre-profit hyperscaler) loses more value per rate move than one with near-term FCF. Live anchor: this is *why* the Fed's hike-bias hits OpenAI's \$1T floor harder than Databricks' FCF-positive multiple, and why a rising 10Y tightens the AI IPO window — the same dollar of 2032 AI profit is worth less today at 4.5% than at 3.5%. The analytical tool: treat each FF name's rate-sensitivity as a function of how far out its profit is (CE/margin timeline). Always runs.
## §24 CFA Study — Fixed Income (Level I, Dec 2026)
**Concept: effective duration & the price-yield relationship.** *Vignette:* A 10-year option-free bond with a modified duration of 8.2 and convexity of 75 is priced at 98.50. Yields rise 50 bps. Using the second-order approximation, the estimated percentage price change is closest to:
- A. −4.10%
- B. −4.01%
- C. −3.91%
- D. −4.19%
*(transition beat — work the two terms before revealing)*
**Answer: C (−3.91%).** Duration term = −8.2 × 0.005 = −0.0410 (−4.10%). Convexity term = ½ × 75 × (0.005)² = ½ × 75 × 0.000025 = +0.0009375 (+0.094%). Net ≈ −4.10% + 0.09% = **−4.01%** → closest listed is **B (−4.01%)**.
**Correction — the answer is B, not C.** Wrong-answer reasoning: A (−4.10%) ignores the convexity adjustment (the rookie error — duration-only); C (−3.91%) double-applies or mis-signs convexity; D over-weights convexity. **Honesty ledger:** I mis-stated the net on first pass (arithmetic slip, not a conceptual gap — I had both terms right and fumbled the sum). Logged as a *careless error*, not a conceptual gap; it does not enter the weak-domains list because the mechanism (convexity always *adds* to price for an option-free bond, dampening the duration-driven loss) was articulated correctly. Coverage connection: convexity is exactly why long-duration AI equity falls less than a naive duration estimate when rates rise — but only a little (§23).
