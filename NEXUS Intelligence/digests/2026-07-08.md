Standing gap backlog item selected:<br><br>\*\*Concept: Commodity Market Microstructure — bid/ask spread as price discovery signal.\*\*<br><br>\*\*Plain definition:\*\* In a commodity market, the bid/ask spread is the difference between the highest price a buyer will pay (bid) and the lowest price a seller will accept (ask). The spread is the market's real-time measure of uncertainty and liquidity — wide spread = high uncertainty or thin liquidity; tight spread = confidence and depth.<br><br>\*\*Mechanism (the why):\*\* Market makers set the spread based on their inventory risk, information asymmetry, and order flow. In a new commodity market (like GPU compute, if Ornn's thesis works), spreads will initially be extremely wide because no one knows the "true" price — there is no futures curve, no settlement price history, and no clearing mechanism. The spread narrows as volume accumulates and price history gives participants anchor points. This is exactly the dynamic that played out in natural gas markets in the early 1990s after deregulation.<br><br>\*\*Live anchor:\*\* Ornn (§4, §12) is explicitly building the infrastructure to tighten the bid/ask spread on GPU compute. Today, that spread is effectively infinite — there is no public price for a GPU-hour on a spot basis; every deal is bilateral and opaque. If Ornn creates a clearing price, the spread tightens, information asymmetry falls, and hyperscaler pricing power erodes.
