## 🎓 13. CFA PREP
**Domain:** Ethics — CFA Institute Standards of Professional Conduct
**Concept:** Standard I(B) — Independence and Objectivity. Analysts must maintain independence from external pressures that could compromise objectivity. The key trap: receipt of gifts, compensation arrangements, or benefits from covered companies creates real or apparent conflicts even when the analyst believes their judgment is unaffected. The exam frequently tests the distinction between modest gifts (acceptable with disclosure) and arrangements that could reasonably be seen as influencing output (must be declined or disclosed to employer).
**Practice Question:** Harrison Rolfes is a senior analyst covering Anthropic. The company's IR team invites him to a 3-day research summit in San Francisco, covers his hotel and meals (\~\$800 value), and provides exclusive briefings on unreleased product roadmap. Harrison discloses the invitation to his employer. His employer approves attendance. Harrison subsequently publishes a bullish update on Anthropic. Which of the following best describes whether Harrison violated Standard I(B)?
A) Harrison did not violate the Standard because he disclosed the invitation and received employer approval.
B) Harrison violated the Standard because any benefit from a covered company creates an automatic conflict.
C) Harrison may have violated the Standard if the exclusive roadmap briefings contained material non-public information.
D) Harrison did not violate the Standard because the total value (\~\$800) falls below the threshold requiring disclosure.
**Answer: C.** The exclusive product roadmap briefings are the material issue — not the hospitality. Disclosure and employer approval address the independence optics of the hospitality, but if the roadmap contained MNPI, Standard II(A) is implicated regardless of I(B) procedures. **Why A fails:** Disclosure + approval satisfies I(B) for the hospitality component but does not create a safe harbor for MNPI exposure. **Why B fails:** The Standard does not create a per se prohibition on all benefits — only those that could reasonably impair independence. The exam frequently uses this distractor to test whether candidates over-apply the Standard. **Why D fails:** There is no explicit dollar threshold in the Standards. The question of what constitutes an acceptable benefit is fact-specific, not rule-based.
**Harrison connection:** Every Anthropic briefing you receive as a PitchBook analyst runs directly through this framework — particularly the Series G investor document access and any pre-IPO data room participation.

## 🔍 15. NARRATIVE STRESS-TEST
**The claim:** The hyperscaler deals are value extraction, not validation — Anthropic has pre-committed \~5.5x one year of 2027 projected revenue to AWS and GCP before filing an S-1.
**The strongest counter:** The contracts almost certainly contain usage minimums, not hard spend floors. If Anthropic's revenue scales to \$55B in 2027 as projected, the \$100B AWS commitment is a 10-year obligation averaging \$10B/year — 18% of 2027 revenue, well within manageable range.
**What the counter actually proves:** It proves the math is not catastrophic under the base case. It does not prove the obligations are flexible. Contract language is unknown.
**Conviction update:** Weakens slightly. The bear case requires the spend commitments to be hard obligations. Until contract flexibility terms are disclosed, medium conviction.
**What would flip conviction:** A single disclosure — contract flexibility terms or usage minimums — either confirms the bear case or drops it entirely.
