# §38 DEEP DIVE — The Permitting Bottleneck
**The claim to correct.** Aggregators (Motley Fool, TradingKey) ran headlines this week that a lawsuit "jeopardizes Anthropic's \$45B contract" with SpaceX. The primary document says something more precise — and the precision is the analytical value.
**What the SEC filing actually says.** SpaceX's S-1/A (SEC EDGAR, CIK 0001181412; filings at /Archives/edgar/data/0001181412/000162828026040364/ and companion .../036936/, .../039276/ — T1) discloses that on **May 6, 2026** the NAACP filed a **preliminary injunction** against SpaceX and **MZX Tech LLC** over the mobile gas turbines powering **Colossus II** (Southaven, Mississippi; permitted by the Mississippi DEQ). The theory: the turbines are operating as **"stationary sources" without proper Clean Air Act permits** — i.e., they were run as "temporary" units but function as permanent generation, which triggers a different (and stricter) permitting regime. SpaceX **accrued a \$399M litigation loss** in Q1 (Mar 31).
**The Colossus 1-vs-2 precision.** The named suit targets Colossus **2** (Southaven). Anthropic's exclusive compute lease — the \~\$45B deal — runs on Colossus **1** (Memphis/Boxtown, TN). So the *named* litigation does **not** directly name Anthropic's facility. The aggregator headline conflates the two. **But** — and this is the real exposure — Colossus 1's turbines *also* ran permit-free under the same "temporary" characterization. The risk to Anthropic is not the named suit; it is that the **same Clean Air Act "stationary source" theory**, if it prevails against Colossus 2, extends cleanly to Colossus 1's identically-situated turbines. The exposure is via the *legal theory*, not the *caption*.
**Why this is a novel risk class.** The consensus frame treats environmental/permitting risk as a *builder's* problem — SpaceX's problem, xAI's problem. The correction: it is a **compute-buyer's** problem too. Anthropic contracted for \~\$45B of compute whose power supply may be operating on a permitting theory that is now being litigated. If an injunction forces the turbines offline pending proper permits, the disruption flows downstream to the buyer's compute availability — a supply-chain risk that has nothing to do with chips, models, or capital, and everything to do with the fact that **the AI compute buildout is racing ahead of environmental permitting.** NY Fed's Williams naming AI-driven power demand as the top inflation factor (§10) is the macro version of the same bottleneck: demand for generation is outrunning the permitted supply of it.
**The through-line to the book.** This is why §13 rotated CEG in: when new gas generation is permitting-constrained and litigated, *permitted baseload* (nuclear) becomes the scarcity asset. The permitting bottleneck is simultaneously (a) an operational risk to Anthropic's compute, (b) a valuation-relevant disclosure that will appear in its Oct S-1 obligations narrative, and (c) a tradable power-scarcity thesis. One primary document, three implications — and a consensus headline that got the facility wrong.
**Falsifiable read:** if Anthropic's Oct S-1 discloses Colossus 1 turbine-permitting as a risk factor (it should, given the Colossus 2 precedent), the market's current "SPCX-only" framing is confirmed too narrow. Watching for it.
