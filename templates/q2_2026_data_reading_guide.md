# How to read the Q2 2026 data - what to look for and what it means

A reader's guide to the outline: for each section, what to look for in the data
and how to interpret it. Read the six lenses first - they apply everywhere.

## The six lenses (apply to every number)

1. **Basis discipline.** Two universes are in play: Monitor (1,556 active /
   $8,496.6B) and the primary tracker (1,743 / $8,232.5B). They differ by
   constituent-inclusion rules, not rounding. Always know which basis a figure
   is on; never put a 1,556 count next to a 1,743 count.
2. **Denominator honesty.** Active-market metrics (step-ups, time-between-rounds)
   describe only companies that RAISED. Roughly half the universe is dormant and
   invisible to them. Always ask: what does this denominator omit?
3. **Fresh vs stale.** A mark's AGE matters as much as its value. The last-round
   post-money is "the last price someone paid," frozen until a new round; a fresh
   model mark updates daily. Decompose any aggregate into tested vs untested.
4. **Concentration.** Aggregates are proxies for a few names (space tech is
   SpaceX; AI is OpenAI/Anthropic/xAI). Always ask: how much of this is ONE
   company?
5. **Composition vs repricing.** When an aggregate moves, decide whether prices
   changed (repricing) or membership changed (names entering/leaving). Never read
   a composition move as a market move.
6. **Provenance.** For every figure: disclosed vs estimated, dated vs undated,
   primary round vs secondary trade. A number's reliability is a function of its
   source, not its precision.

## Market overview (leaders)

### The quarter that belonged to five companies
- **Look for:** the top-5 and top-10 share of the quarter's value, and the
  single-largest strip-out; new-unicorn count against the value those new names
  represent.
- **Means:** high concentration (top-5 ~65%+) means the headline describes a few
  frontier labs' fundraising cadence, not "the market." The strip-out shows the
  genuine broad figure underneath. Many new unicorns but little value = the $1B
  bar got easier to clear (check-size inflation), not a broad re-rating.

### Fresh vs stale prices + secondary validation
- **Look for:** per name, the fresh model mark (daily) vs the last-round
  post-money, the % gap, and where actual secondary trades sit relative to both.
- **Means:** a positive gap (fresh > stale) says the model thinks the company
  appreciated since its last round - the stale mark understates. A negative gap
  is repricing risk. The secondary tape is the arbiter: trades clustering near
  the fresh mark validate the model; trades near the stale round suggest the
  model is optimistic. Three prices agreeing = a reliable mark; diverging = the
  number is a guess. This is the whole "are private marks real" question, made
  measurable.

### Value creation at the top vs stagnation everywhere else
- **Look for:** value created since first round (fresh mark minus cumulative
  invested capital) and per year; the spread top-to-bottom; the top-3 share of
  all value creation.
- **Means:** separates companies that COMPOUNDED value from those that merely
  RAISED a lot. A huge valuation on huge capital is not the same as a franchise
  built on little. Value/year normalizes for age. If the top 3 hold most
  cumulative value creation, the "boom" is a handful of franchises and a
  stagnant middle.

### Step-ups: the active market is functioning
- **Look for:** median step-up (new post / prior post) by year vs the ~1.9x
  2016-2018 baseline and the ~2.6x 2021 peak; down-round frequency and share of
  value.
- **Means:** ~2x = a functioning, disciplined market; >2.6x = bubble; <1.5x =
  stress. The critical caveat (lens 2): step-ups only see companies that raised.
  A healthy step-up says "the active market works," NOT "the universe is
  healthy" - the dormant half is not in the number.

### AI: one-third of count, nearly half of value
- **Look for:** AI's share of count vs share of value, the trajectory, the 50%
  crossing; and concentration WITHIN AI (top-2/3 names' share of AI value).
- **Means:** value share above count share means AI companies are individually
  bigger (mega-marks). Crossing 50% value = AI is the majority of private tech
  value. But within-AI concentration reveals "AI exposure" is exposure to two or
  three cap tables, not a diversified sector. The trap is treating AI as a broad
  allocation when it is a few names.

## Verticals

### SaaS and AI dominance, and the drop-off
- **Look for:** count and aggregate by vertical, ranked; the steep fall after the
  top two.
- **Means:** SaaS (~$2.4T) and AI (~$4.1T) dwarf everything else. Nonexclusive
  tagging (1,793 tags / $11,857B, above the universe) means a company counts in
  several verticals - do not sum verticals to a universe total. The market is
  really two themes plus a long tail.

### The narrow investable universe
- **Look for:** unique/dedup company counts per vertical; what a 20-name
  portfolio would actually hold.
- **Means:** the tag count overstates breadth. Dedup shows how few distinct
  companies exist per theme, and how much SaaS/AI overlap dominates. "Breadth" is
  largely an artifact of multi-tagging.

### Count and valuation vs performance: the vertical return layer
- **Look for:** each vertical index's total return, annualized return, and risk,
  set beside its size; always check the return WINDOW (the summary block is
  6/16/2023 onward; compute from the fuller level history where it exists).
- **Means:** adds what size hides. A large vertical can be flat (value sitting in
  old marks); a small one can be compounding. Return = how constituents actually
  performed (mark-to-market); risk = volatility. The size-vs-performance quadrant
  tells you where value is live vs stale.

### AI as growth engine and concentration risk
- **Look for:** AI as simultaneously the highest-formation, highest-value-growth,
  and highest-attrition vertical.
- **Means:** AI is both the reason to be in the market and the reason to worry.
  Underweight it and you bet against the market's center of gravity; overweight
  it and you concentrate in a few cap tables. This paradox is the report's core.

### The 2021 vintage and 2028 outlook
- **Look for:** the 2021 cohort's survivors, the share of its valuation set before
  2023 and never retested, and a 20% / 30% markdown scenario on the untested
  marks.
- **Means:** 2021 minted the most unicorns ever at peak prices. Marks set then
  and never retested are "untested valuations." Fund-cycle math (10-year 2021
  funds mature 2028-2031) forces resolution - markdown, M&A, or wind-down. The
  scenario range quantifies the aggregate's exposure to that reckoning.

## PBQ and valuations (top 20 = the Unicorn 20 constituents)

### Why the valuation table isn't a quality ranking
- **Look for:** the capital-efficiency spread across the 20 (value created per $
  invested) - expect an order-of-magnitude range.
- **Means:** the valuation rank is a list of the most EXPENSIVE companies, not
  the best. Two names at the same valuation can differ wildly in capital
  efficiency - one built a franchise on little, one is buying scale. The table
  treats them as peers; quality separates them.

### Expanding PBQ + secondary as the estimate-integrity input
- **Look for:** the five PBQ dimensions across the 20, and which are
  data-computable (capital efficiency, estimate integrity) vs analyst-supplied
  (revenue quality, compute independence, governance, moat).
- **Means:** PBQ measures business quality independent of price. Estimate
  integrity = do the three prices (model, round, secondary) agree? Agreement =
  a reliable mark; divergence = an uncertain valuation, itself a
  governance/quality signal. It operationalizes how much to trust the number.

### PBQ per $ of valuation
- **Look for:** quality score per $B of post-money (or per valuation rank), as a
  ranked bar.
- **Means:** who delivers the most business quality per dollar of price paid -
  the "best value" ranking, the inverse of the valuation table. This is the only
  cleared expression of quality-vs-price (no scatter or correlation, per the
  standing embargo).

### PBQ scorecard by company
- **Look for:** composite plus the five sub-scores; the radar shape per company.
- **Means:** the profile shows HOW a company earns its score - high capital
  efficiency but weak governance, say, vs balanced. The radar exposes strengths
  and weaknesses the composite hides.

## Valuations and fallen unicorns

### Where the value sits
- **Look for:** the top-10 share of aggregate valuation over time and its
  reconcentration.
- **Means:** the top-10 share bottomed near 18.4% in 2023 (the broad-based era)
  and has reconcentrated toward 2016 levels. Rising = value re-concentrating in
  megacaps while the middle lags. It tells you whether value creation is broad or
  narrow.

### Latest valuation
- **Look for:** the aggregate and its QoQ move, decomposed into repricing vs
  composition; the fresh per-name marks for the 20.
- **Means:** the aggregate can move because marks changed OR because names entered
  or left (SpaceX's status, new formation). Decomposing (lens 5) stops you
  reading a composition change as a market move. The 20's fresh marks are the
  leading edge of where marks are actually going.

### The coverage gap: stale marks vs fresh marks
- **Look for:** the share of aggregate on marks older than 24 months vs recent;
  the block with no estimate at all; the estimate-vs-mark direction (% above /
  flat / below).
- **Means:** the credibility test for the $8.5T headline. If most value sits on
  2+ year-old marks, the aggregate is a museum of old prices. A high share
  estimated above last mark (the up-round-pressure read) says marks understate -
  but estimates are undated, so treat it as a static cross-section, not a trend.
  The uncovered block is where repricing risk hides. The 20 are the fresh-mark
  frontier; most of the universe is not.

### Step-ups and relative velocity of value creation (RVVC)
- **Look for:** median step-up AND RVVC (new valuation created per $ invested),
  read together, by year. (RVVC needs deal amounts, from the primary tracker.)
- **Means:** step-up can look healthy while RVVC is poor - a company raises a huge
  round for a small valuation bump (steps up in price, destroys value per
  dollar). RVVC below 1 = diluting to survive, not growing. The recovery off the
  2023 trough (~0.74 baseline) tells you whether the active market creates real
  value or just reinflates. The pair is more honest than step-up alone.

### Fallen unicorns
- **Look for:** fallen count per year and per vertical, median down-round
  post-money, Q2 additions; the shadow inventory of dormant untested names; the
  cohort-survival split (share never re-raising at 6..60 months, AI vs non-AI).
- **Means:** fallen = lost $1B status (markdown, shutdown, sub-$1B exit). The
  count UNDERSTATES stress because it captures only disclosed failures - the
  dormant untested population is the shadow inventory, and if 10-20% resolve
  down, the true count roughly doubles. Cohort survival (AI re-raising more
  reliably than non-AI) shows AI's follow-on edge even at the unicorn tier - the
  payoff of the dormancy watch.

## The through-line

Every section is one argument in different clothes: the $8.5T headline is real
but concentrated, fresh at the top and stale in the middle, and the gap between
the tested few and the untested many is the risk the next three years resolve.
Read every number for which side of that divide it describes.
