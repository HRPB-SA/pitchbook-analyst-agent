# Global Unicorn Tracker - Q2 2026

Late-Stage Company Research | As of June 30, 2026

Primary basis: PitchBook deal-level unicorn tracker (1,743 active / $8.23T
aggregate). The Unicorn 20 daily model marks and the Unicorn Monitor are used
only where flagged, for the three-mark secondary validation and the vertical
index returns, and are never blended with the primary-tracker figures.

---

## The one-line read

The unicorn universe closed Q2 at $8.23T across 1,743 companies, +13% YoY, and
almost none of that growth was broad. AI added $1.59T of aggregate value in six
months against a total universe gain of $0.93T, which means every non-AI vertical
shrank in aggregate, by roughly $0.66T combined. The market did not expand in Q2.
It concentrated onto a dozen AI cap tables, and the rest of this report is the
mechanics of that concentration and what it does to marks, exits, and risk.

---

## Market overview

### Five companies took the quarter

Q2 fundraising was five deals and a long tail. The top five rounds totaled $87.1B,
and Anthropic alone was $65B of that, a $65B raise into a $965B post-money that
ran 74.6% of the top five and 5.4x the second-largest deal (Prometheus, $12B into
$41B). Anduril ($5B into $61B), ByteDance ($3B into $370B), and Isomorphic Labs
($2.1B) filled out the group. Four of the five are AI. Anthropic by itself was
16.4% of all first-half deal value ($65B of $396.1B).

The word "five" is literal, not shorthand. Deal size falls under $2B by the sixth
name and under $1B almost immediately after. The top ten reached 68.9% of sized
deal value and the top 25 hit 78.5%. So the quarter's fundraising headline
describes five specific cap tables, and any read on "Q2 unicorn financing" is a
read on Anthropic with rounding attached. For allocators, the practical point is
that late-stage AI primary supply is now gated by a handful of frontier labs
setting terms the rest of the market prices off of.

### The marks at the top are the freshest and the least corroborated

Twenty marquee names carry three prices each: a daily model mark (Unicorn 20), a
last-round post-money (Monitor), and a secondary-implied value from actual trades
(Monitor). In aggregate the model sits +15.2% above the last round for the 18
names with a priced round, which a rising market should produce. The disagreement
shows up where the capital sits. Secondary trades print below the model for 12 of
the 20, and far below for the two largest at the 6/30 close: SpaceX -32% (a $650B
gap between a $2.03T model mark and a $1.38T secondary), Anthropic -22% (~$282B),
OpenAI -9%, Databricks -7%. Both top marks caught a fresh event, SpaceX's June IPO
and an Anthropic round, and ran ahead of a secondary tape that had not repriced.

Below the megacaps the sign flips. Secondaries trade above the last round for
Applied Intuition (+36%), Anysphere (+30%), Anduril (+22%), and Revolut (+16%),
so those stale marks understate the companies rather than inflate them. At the
bottom, all three prices have already reset down together (Ripple, Epic Games,
Kraken). The finding an LP should carry out of this: mark reliability is inverse
to position size in Q2. The largest holdings are the ones where the model and the
tape disagree most, so any portfolio marked to model is carrying its biggest
names at the widest spread to where stock actually changes hands.

### The average is a lie the median exposes

Median unicorn post-money is $2.0B. The average is $18.19B. That 9.1x mean-to-
median ratio was 3.1x in 2025 and 2.8x in 2024, so the distribution did not drift,
it snapped. Round sizes tell the same story: a $200M median deal against a $1,143M
average, a 5.7x skew and the widest in eleven years. The $8.23T aggregate rose
because a dozen marks re-rated, not because the median company moved, and the two
numbers now describe different markets. Anyone quoting the aggregate as a
market-wide signal is reading the top twelve names and calling it the universe.

### Step-ups say the active market cleared, and cleared up

For companies that actually raised, terms firmed. The median step-up hit 2.20x in
2026, up from 2.05x in 2025 and the highest since the 2.64x peak of 2021. Median
time between rounds fell to 0.98yr from 1.21yr in 2025 and 1.45yr in 2024, so the
gap between raises is compressing back toward boom-era speed. Down rounds went
close to zero, six in the first half worth $3.85B against 31 full-year in 2025 and
34 in 2024. The velocity, though, lives at the bottom of the size ladder, not the
top: the step-up leaders are Hark (151.4x), Blitzy (30x), and SendCutSend (30x),
all sub-$7B, while the giants stepped up in dollars but not in multiple. Value is
concentrated at the top; velocity is concentrated at the bottom. And the metric
only sees companies that raised, which is well under half the book, so a healthy
step-up describes the active market, not the universe.

### AI crossed from a third to three-fifths

AI is now 702 of 1,743 unicorns (40.3% by count) and $5.08T of $8.23T (61.7% by
value). The 21-point gap between value share and count share is the whole point:
AI companies are individually far larger than the universe average. The slope is
steep, AI aggregate value ran from $3.49T at end-2025 to $5.08T, +45.6% in six
months, and AI took 109 of the 178 first-half mints (61%). Concentration is
heavier in the flow than in the stock, so AI's share is still climbing. Two
narrower cuts exist and should not be confused with this one: the AI vertical tag
(865 companies, nonexclusive) and the Monitor curated AI index (430 / $4,143.7B /
48.8%). This section runs on the 702-company aggregate throughout.

## Verticals

### Two themes and a tail

On tagged value, SaaS leads at $6.44T (968 companies) and AI is second at $6.02T
(865), with FinTech a distant third at $2.02T (436). Then the drop: Space
Technology $1.87T (34), Mobility $0.83T, CloudTech $0.74T, HealthTech $0.45T. The
top two verticals hold roughly $12.5T of tagged value between them; from the
fourth down, each is a rounding difference against AI and SaaS. Tags are
nonexclusive, so vertical counts sum past 1,743 and vertical values sum to about
1.5x the universe, and they must never be added to a total. A company building
AI-powered SaaS books in both leaders, which means the breadth on a vertical map
is mostly a tagging artifact rather than a real opportunity set.

Value per company exposes where a vertical is one name in disguise. Space
Technology averages ~$55B across 34 companies ($1.87T), but that average is
essentially SpaceX; remove it and the vertical is unremarkable. Treat any vertical
aggregate as a candidate for single-name capture before citing it as a theme.

### The investable set collapses to a few dozen names

Of 1,743 unicorns, very few clear the filters that matter to a real allocator: a
recent mark, size, and secondary liquidity. Value is top-heavy (the top ten carry
a large share of the $8.23T while the median company is $2.0B), and stacking three
filters, a mark under 12 months old, size above $5B, and any secondary market,
takes the list from 1,743 to a few dozen. Diversifying across the unicorn universe
is largely unavailable as a strategy; the accessible book is a concentrated,
AI-heavy, US-domiciled set, and a vertical-blind portfolio drawn from it is a
SaaS-and-AI portfolio whether or not that was the intent.

### Size and return do not line up

The biggest verticals are not the fastest. On 2026 medians, AI ran RVVC of 1.84x
and a 2.35x step-up across 249 deals, and SaaS 1.42x and 2.18x across 176, both
healthy but mid-pack on velocity. The fastest appreciators are smaller: Robotics
and Drones posted the top median RVVC at 2.03x (5.66x average), and CleanTech ran
hot at 3.30x RVVC and a 2.77x step-up. Crypto cooled hard, median RVVC down from
2.62x to 1.11x. At the bottom, Cybersecurity (0.66x) and E-Commerce (0.38x) are
stepping rounds up in price while creating less than a dollar of value per dollar
in, the classic tell of raises that flatter valuation without building it.

The three-year mark-to-market layer confirms the hierarchy (Monitor vertical
indexes, annualized): AI +58.1%/yr (+302.6% cumulative), Enterprise SaaS +34.2%,
Cybersecurity +22.2%, the only three verticals to beat the Global TME public
benchmark of +19.6%/yr (+72.4% cumulative). AI compounded at roughly 3x the public
rate. FinTech, third-largest by value, returned +12.9% and sits below the
benchmark, heavy in stock and weak in flow. One methodology flag for any
risk-adjusted use: these returns run on infrequently updated private marks, so the
measured volatility is suppressed and any Sharpe built on it overstates the asset
class.

### AI is the engine and the single point of failure

AI is the entire growth case and the entire concentration risk, and those are the
same names. It drove +$1.59T of a +$0.93T universe move, so the rest of the market
contracted around it; it is 62% of value, four of the top five rounds, and 61% of
new mints. Because AI marks move together and carry the index, a re-rate in a few
frontier names moves the whole aggregate. Size the tail: a 25% markdown of AI
marks removes ~$1.27T from the universe, 15.4% of all private tech value, from one
theme repricing. Underweighting AI bets against nearly two-thirds of value;
overweighting it concentrates into cap tables the secondary tape already marks
20-30% below their models. There is no clean hedge inside the asset class, because
the growth and the risk do not separate.

### What the 2021 cohort says about the AI class of 2026

The 2021 vintage is the cleanest forward analog. Of 635 unicorns minted that year,
five years on 470 remain active (74%), 102 exited (16%), and 62 impaired: 34 fell
below $1B while still private, 15 fell at exit (a down-IPO or down-M&A below
unicorn status), and 13 went bankrupt. Two readings matter. First, three-quarters
of the cohort are still illiquid four and a half years in, which is what a 8.47-yr
median time to exit produces. Second, 15 companies crossed out below their unicorn
mark, a failure mode the headline exit count hides entirely. Run that curve
forward onto today's 702 AI unicorns and the base case is a quarter impaired and
three-quarters still illiquid into the next decade, with the added hazard that AI
marks are more correlated and more concentrated than 2021's were. The ten-year
2021 funds mature 2028-2031, which puts a clock on the untested marks: they resolve
by markdown, M&A, or wind-down on that schedule whether or not the market
cooperates.

## Deal dynamics, capital, and exits

### Fewer rounds, larger syndicates, thinner participation

Round structure crowded even as round count fell. Six-or-more-investor syndicates
are 60.9% of 2026 known-investor rounds, near the 2021 peak of 64%. But
participation breadth is not recovering with the dollars: quarterly investor
participation peaked at 2,892 in Q3 2021, bottomed at 895 in Q4 2023, recovered to
1,415 in Q1 2026, then fell back to 995 in Q2 even with deal value high. Fewer,
bigger, more crowded rounds, funded by a short list, Sequoia (491 unicorn deals
since 2016), Tiger Global (472), Andreessen Horowitz (454), Accel (382), Coatue
(344). The investor concentration mirrors the company concentration: a narrow set
of names underwrites a narrow set of cap tables.

### The exit window reopened, and it is one company

2026 exit value hit $2,110.9B across 50 exits, the strongest in the dataset, and
80% of it is a single event. SpaceX's IPO was $1,690B on its own; strip it and the
$420.7B ex-SpaceX figure is still the best exit year since 2021 but a fraction of
the headline. Both numbers travel together or the read is wrong. Public listings
drove 85% of exit value, and the window that shut for three years is open again,
SpaceX, Cerebras ($34B), and Quantinuum ($14B) all listed in the quarter. The
counterweight is duration: median time to exit rose to 8.47yr (a 9.17-yr peak in
2025), so the names clearing now are old vintages draining a backlog, not fresh
2023-2024 companies. The exit market is emptying a reservoir, not running current,
and once the pre-2021 backlog clears, exit value normalizes far below the 2026
headline.

## Business quality (PBQ / AIBQ) and valuations

### The price ladder is not a quality ranking

The naive ranking sorts by post-money: Anthropic, OpenAI, ByteDance, Stripe,
Databricks, Waymo, down the line. That orders companies by expense, and price is a
function of capital raised as much as of value created; two names at the same
valuation can differ by an order of magnitude in capital efficiency. It is also
part staleness, two of the top ten ride marks more than a year old that a fresh
round could move either way. A quality-adjusted ranking breaks the rank order of
the price ladder, which is exactly why PBQ belongs beside price, not beneath it.

### What the data can score, and what it cannot

A full PBQ or AIBQ composite needs operating inputs, revenue quality, margin,
customer concentration, compute independence, governance, moat, that none of the
three files carry. Composites exist for six frontier names only (Databricks 8.7,
Anthropic 7.7, SpaceX 7.5, OpenAI 4.2, xAI 3.8, SSI 2.3), and the spread is the
argument for the framework: OpenAI, the third most valuable company on earth,
scores below the midpoint. The one quality dimension this data does supply is
estimate integrity, the agreement among a name's three marks. Tight clusters
(Canva, Stripe, Rippling) carry corroborated, reliable valuations; wide divergence
(Neuralink at +441% model over round, Ripple, Kraken) carries low mark
reliability, itself a governance signal. The honest next step is to flag which of
the 20 have real secondary liquidity versus which are marks only. The full
scorecard and a PBQ-per-dollar ranking, quality bought per dollar of price, which
would invert the ladder, both wait on analyst composites and should be scoped as
pending, not fabricated from this data.

## Valuations and fallen unicorns

### The distribution is power-law, not normal

Two companies, Anthropic ($965B) and OpenAI ($852B), hold $1.82T, 22% of the
entire $8.23T universe, and the top ten hold a majority, against a $2.0B median.
Concentration this extreme decouples the index from the median: the aggregate
climbs on a dozen marks while the typical unicorn sits still. A cumulative-share
curve is the right exhibit, and the one-number version is that a handful of names
carry a share of value no normal distribution could generate. Treat the index and
the median as two separate instruments, because in 2026 they are.

### Freshness is good at the very top and decays fast below it

Anthropic ($965B, May 2026), ByteDance ($370B, May 2026), and OpenAI ($852B, Mar
2026) carry current marks, but the freshness falls off quickly, with top-ten names
still on 2023 marks (a mid-2023 FinTech mark, a May 2023 SHEIN mark). The hard
measure, computed from actual latest-round dates across the active book, is that
47.6% of unicorns have not raised in more than 24 months and 37.1% in more than 36
months. That is the credibility test for the $8.23T headline: close to half of it
rests on marks untested in a financing for two years or more. The universe is two
tiers, a fresh-marked frontier of a few dozen names and a large stale-marked
middle whose repricing risk is invisible in the aggregate, and the aggregate
should be split into fresh-marked and stale-marked dollars before it is quoted.
(This 47.6% figure comes from latest-round dates and supersedes an earlier
qualifying-date proxy of 78.5% that overstated staleness.)

### Velocity is back, and it is concentrated

Median RVVC recovered to 1.42x in 2026 from 0.93x in 2025 and a 0.23x trough in
2023, back above the ~0.73x pre-boom baseline, while the 2.20x median step-up and
0.98-yr cadence confirm rounds clearing up and fast. RVVC above 1.0 means each
raised dollar created more than a dollar of new valuation, the reverse of the 2023
dilution regime. The caveat is the same skew that runs the whole report: average
RVVC is 2.73x against the 1.42x median, so value creation, like value itself, sits
in a few names, and the metric only counts companies that raised.

### Falls slowed to a trickle, and that is not the same as health

There are 245 fallen unicorns to date, but the flow of new falls collapsed: six
down rounds in the first half of 2026 worth $3.85B, against a 34-fall peak in 2024.
When companies fall, they fall hard, the median down-round post-money is $524.5M
and the median markdown is 67% below the prior peak, so the typical fallen unicorn
lands at roughly a third of its high-water mark, just under the $1B line. The 2021
cohort carries the underreported detail: 15 of its failures fell at exit, crossing
out below unicorn status through a down-IPO or down-M&A that the exit count books
as a success. The low current fall rate should not read as strength, because the
47.6% of the book on untested marks is the shadow inventory the next wave comes
from, and a slow fall count in a market this stale is a timing signal, not an
all-clear.

## What to watch

The $8.23T is real, concentrated, and mostly untested. Concentrated at the top
(two names hold 22%, the top five took 62% of Q2 deal flow), fresh only at that
top (marquee marks corroborated by secondaries to within ~10-30%), stale across
the middle (47.6% untested for two years or more), and above all an AI event, AI
added more value in six months than the whole universe did and is now three-fifths
of all private tech value on two-fifths of the names. The active market functions
again (2.2x step-ups, 1.42x RVVC, down rounds near zero), the IPO window reopened
(though 80% of exit value is one SpaceX listing), and falls slowed, but the 2021
vintage shows the other side of a boom: a quarter impaired, three-quarters still
illiquid five years on, and a fund clock that forces resolution between 2028 and
2031.

The number to track is not the aggregate, which will keep climbing on a dozen AI
marks. It is the spread between the tested few and the untested many, and the
tell that closes it either way is whether AI's marks hold when the next rounds
price. The secondary tape already says the biggest ones are rich by 20-30%. If the
next Anthropic or SpaceX round prints below the model rather than above it, the
re-rate does not stay contained to one cap table, because on these correlations a
25% AI markdown is $1.27T off the top of the universe.
